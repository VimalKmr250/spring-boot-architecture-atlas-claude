What changes with the level

The questions barely change between mid-level, senior and staff loops. The bar does. Interviewers calibrate on the scope of what you reason about — one component, one system, or several systems and the teams that own them — far more than on vocabulary.

SignalMid-levelSeniorStaff / principal
ScopeOne component, built correctlyA system, including how it failsSeveral systems, and the organisation that builds them
Trade-offsKnows the optionsPicks one, names its cost, and says what would change the choiceFrames the decision so that others can make it; separates reversible choices from irreversible ones
FailureHandles errors when promptedVolunteers the failure mode and the recoveryDesigns the operating model: SLOs, on-call, blast radius
Evidence"I've used it""We ran it at this volume, and this is what broke""We changed how several teams do this, and this is how we measured it"

One question at three levels: "a downstream service is slow"

Mid-level

"I'd add a timeout and a retry, and wrap the call in a Resilience4j circuit breaker."

Correct, and it would pass a mid-level bar. What is missing is why each piece is there and what happens when it fires.

Senior

"Timeout first, sized from the dependency's p99 and our own latency budget. Retries only on idempotent calls, with jitter. A breaker with a fallback we have agreed with product. A bulkhead so it cannot take all our threads. And an alert when the breaker opens."

Staff

All of the senior answer, then: "Why is this synchronous at all? If it is not on the critical path of the user's action, it should be an event. And across the organisation, a shared client library with these defaults, so the next forty services don't each rediscover them."

Aim one level up, not twoAnswer at the level you are interviewing for, and reach one level higher where it comes naturally. A senior candidate who closes with the shared-library point is showing staff-level reach. A mid-level candidate who opens with organisation design has skipped the part that is being assessed.
