Moving an existing system to events

You almost never build event-driven from scratch — you convert something. Six phases, each shipping value on its own, none requiring a big bang.

1–2 Prepare

  • Map the synchronous calls and classify each: must succeed now, or a reaction? Only the second group moves.
  • Add the outbox to the producing service and publish events alongside the existing calls. Nothing consumes them yet.
  • Verify the event stream matches reality — counts, totals, timing — before anyone depends on it.

3–4 Shift one consumer

  • Pick the least critical reaction — search indexing, analytics, notifications.
  • Build the consumer; run it in parallel with the synchronous call, writing to a shadow table.
  • Compare outputs for a week. Then switch with a flag and remove the synchronous call.
  • Repeat for the next consumer. One at a time.

5 Harden

  • Idempotency and a DLT on every consumer before the second one goes live, not after.
  • Trace propagation across the hop — you will need it during phase 6.
  • Reconciliation job comparing source of truth to every consumer's state.
  • Runbook and alerts per flow.

6 Only then, the interesting parts

  • Projections and read models, once the stream is trusted.
  • A saga, once more than one service reacts and someone asks where things are stuck.
  • Streams, once you have a genuine stateful or windowed requirement.

Teams that start at phase 6 are the ones that end up with an event-driven system they cannot debug.

The parallel-run is the whole safety mechanismRunning the event path and the synchronous path together, comparing outputs, and only then switching is what makes this reversible at every step. It costs double processing for a few weeks. That is far cheaper than discovering in production that your event carries a subtly different meaning from the call it replaced.
