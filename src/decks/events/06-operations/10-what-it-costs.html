What an event-driven system costs to run

The line items that appear on the bill and in the on-call rota. Useful for a business case, and for answering "why is this more expensive than the monolith".

Worked monthly estimate

Volume
20,000 events/s × 1 KB ≈ 50 TB/month written
Replication
× 3 for RF=3 → 150 TB of writes
Storage
7 days retained, lz4 at ~4× → ~9 TB on disk
Read amplification
5 consumer groups → 5× read traffic, and cross-AZ reads are charged
Brokers
6 instances sized for network, not CPU
Plus
schema registry, Connect cluster, monitoring, and the consumers themselves

Divide by orders processed and you have cost per 1,000 orders for the event path — directly comparable to the synchronous alternative.

The costs that surprise people

  • Cross-AZ traffic. Replication and consumption across zones is charged per gigabyte and can exceed the compute bill. Rack-aware follower fetching helps.
  • Retention creep. "Let's keep 30 days just in case" quadruples storage and nobody revisits it.
  • Read amplification. Each new consumer group re-reads everything. Ten groups is 10× read cost.
  • Idle consumers. Pods beyond the partition count cost money and process nothing.
  • On-call. A broker estate needs owners, upgrades and a rota. Often the largest real cost.
Three levers that actually move the billCompression — lz4 or zstd typically cuts storage and network 3–5× for a little CPU. Retention per topic — most topics do not need seven days; some need thirty. Decide each one. Tiered storage — long retention on object storage costs a fraction of broker disk, which changes the replay conversation entirely.
