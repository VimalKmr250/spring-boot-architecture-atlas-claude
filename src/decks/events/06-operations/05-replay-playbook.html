Replay and reprocessing: the playbook

Replay is the superpower of a log-based system and the fastest way to cause a second incident. Four scenarios, each with a different safe procedure.

1 · Drain a DLT after fixing a bug

  1. Fix and deploy the consumer first. Confirm new traffic is clean.
  2. Consume the DLT with a purpose-built job, not the main listener.
  3. Republish to the main topic, or apply directly — whichever preserves idempotency.
  4. Rate-limit. Ten thousand dead letters replayed at full speed becomes a second outage.

2 · Rebuild a projection

  1. Build into a new table with a new consumer group.
  2. Let it catch up; compare against the live table.
  3. Switch reads with a feature flag.
  4. Keep the old table until you are confident.

Never reset the live group and truncate in place — you have no read model at all while it rebuilds.

3 · Backfill a new consumer

  1. Deploy with auto.offset.reset=earliest and a new group id.
  2. Apply a client quota first, so the backfill cannot starve production consumers.
  3. Expect old schema versions — your upcasters must cover them.
  4. Watch downstream systems: replaying a year of events into a database at full speed will find its limits.

4 · Reprocess after a logic bug

  1. Work out what the wrong logic already did. Emails sent, payments taken — replay will not unsend them.
  2. Reset the group to a timestamp, not an offset — timestamps are meaningful to humans and stable across partitions.
  3. Suppress non-idempotent side effects for the replay window, with a flag.
  4. Reconcile afterwards and report the delta.
Resetting a group — the group must be inactive
# Always --dry-run first. It prints what WOULD happen.
kafka-consumer-groups.sh --bootstrap-server $B --group projection-order-history \
  --topic orders.order.placed.v1 --reset-offsets \
  --to-datetime 2026-09-14T00:00:00.000 --dry-run

# Then --execute. Other options: --to-earliest --to-latest --shift-by -1000
#   --to-offset N   --by-duration PT6H
The rule that prevents the second incidentReplay re-fires every side effect the handler performs. Customers have been emailed their order confirmation four times by a well-intentioned replay. Before any replay, list what the consumer does that is not idempotent, and turn it off for the duration — deliberately, with a flag, and tell the people who own it.
