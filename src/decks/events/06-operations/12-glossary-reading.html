Glossary and where to go deeper

The terms that come up in design reviews and interviews, and the sources worth the time.

Terms

Offset
A record's position in a partition. A consumer group's committed offset is its bookmark.
ISR
In-sync replicas — those caught up with the leader. acks=all means all of these.
Consumer group
Both the parallelism unit and the isolation boundary. One partition to one member at a time.
Rebalance
Reassigning partitions when membership changes. Cooperative and KIP-848 make it nearly free.
Compaction
Retain the latest value per key forever. Turns a topic into a snapshot.
Tombstone
A null value on a compacted topic — the key is deleted.
Outbox
Write the event to your database in the same transaction; relay it afterwards.
Saga
A sequence of local transactions with compensating actions instead of a rollback.
Projection
A read model maintained by consuming events. Rebuildable by replay.
Upcaster
Converts an old event version to the current shape at the consumer boundary.
EOS
Exactly-once semantics — within Kafka. Elsewhere, idempotency.
Backpressure
What a system does when it cannot keep up. In Kafka, the topic absorbs it until retention does not.

Going deeper

  • Kleppmann, Designing Data-Intensive Applications — chapter 11 on streams is the best explanation of logs, ordering and derived data anywhere.
  • Stopford, Designing Event-Driven Systems — free from Confluent; the enterprise patterns, told well.
  • Hohpe & Woolf, Enterprise Integration Patterns — the vocabulary everyone still uses, and it predates all of this.
  • Richardson, Microservices Patterns — the definitive treatment of sagas and the outbox.
  • Vernon, Implementing Domain-Driven Design — where domain events come from and what makes one well-formed.
  • The Kafka documentation's design section — genuinely well written, and shorter than people expect.
The three sentences worth being able to say"We design for at-least-once delivery into idempotent consumers, which gives exactly-once effects." · "Every event that must not be lost goes through an outbox, because there is no transaction across the database and the broker." · "Ordering is guaranteed per partition, so we key on the entity whose sequence matters." Those three cover most of what an interviewer is checking for, and most of what a design review needs to establish.
