Securing an event platform

Four layers, and the one that gets skipped is authorisation — a cluster where every application can read every topic is a data-governance finding waiting to be written.

LayerMechanismProtects againstNote
Encryption in transitTLS on every listenerNetwork interceptionTable stakes. Costs some CPU unless offloaded

ACLs: least privilege, per application
# inventory service: read orders, write its own events, use its own group
kafka-acls --add --allow-principal User:inventory-service \
  --operation Read --topic orders.order.placed.v1
kafka-acls --add --allow-principal User:inventory-service \
  --operation Read --group inventory-order-reservation
kafka-acls --add --allow-principal User:inventory-service \
  --operation Write --topic inventory.stock.reserved.v1

# Deny by default at the cluster level:
#   allow.everyone.if.no.acl.found = false
# Anything not explicitly granted is refused — the setting that makes ACLs mean something.
Personal data in events is foreverA customer's name and address in a topic with 90-day retention exists in 90 days of segments, in every consumer's local copy, in the lake, and in every backup. A deletion request cannot reach any of it.

Two defences: minimise — put ids in events and let consumers look up what they are entitled to — or crypto-shred: encrypt personal fields with a per-subject key and delete the key. The events remain; their contents become unreadable.

The quiet risk of a shared clusterA topic with no ACL on a permissive cluster is readable by every application in the estate, including ones from other departments with different data-handling obligations. In a bank that is a control failure, not a configuration preference — and it is invisible until an audit.
Principals from the platformUse mTLS certificates issued by a service mesh or a workload-identity system rather than long-lived SCRAM passwords in config. Rotation stops being a project, and a compromised credential expires on its own.
