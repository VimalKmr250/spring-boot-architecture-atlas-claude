Three kinds of message, and why confusing them hurts

"We publish events" is often untrue — many teams publish commands over a topic and inherit the worst of both models. The distinction is not pedantry; it decides coupling, ownership and error handling.

The smell: a command dressed as an eventSendConfirmationEmail on a topic is a command. It has exactly one legitimate consumer, the producer knows what will happen, and if it fails someone must be told. Publishing it as an "event" means nobody owns the failure and the producer has coupling it cannot see.

Publish OrderPlaced instead and let the notification service decide that an email is the right reaction. Now a second reaction can be added without the producer changing.

Commands over a broker are still legitimate

Sending a command to a durable queue gives you retry, backpressure and decoupling in time without decoupling in knowledge. That is a normal and useful pattern — a work queue.

Just be honest that it is a command: one consumer group, a clear owner, and an error path that reaches a human.

The testAsk: "if nobody consumed this, would anything be wrong?" Yes → it is a command, and it needs an owner and an SLA. No → it is an event, and you are free to fan out.
