Events across regions

The event equivalent of the multi-region question in the architecture deck, and the answer follows the same logic: decide where writes happen, and the rest falls out.

Regional ownership preferred

Each region owns a partition of the business — EU orders in the EU cluster, US orders in the US cluster. Events replicate outward for read-only consumers (analytics, global search) but each entity has exactly one writing region.

  • No conflicts, because no key is written in two places.
  • Ordering per key is preserved, because it is produced in one place.
  • Residency rules are natural to express.

The hard part is routing: a customer must reach the region that owns their data, which is a gateway and identity concern, not a Kafka one.

Global topics with bidirectional replication

Both regions produce to the "same" logical topic, replicated in both directions with prefixes to avoid cycles. Consumers read both.

  • Any region can accept any write — highest availability.
  • Ordering across regions is gone. Two events for one order produced in two regions have no defined order.
  • Conflict resolution becomes a domain rule you must write down.

Only take this on with a genuine active-active requirement and a domain where conflicts are resolvable — last-write-wins on a payment is not acceptable.

Cross-region replication is a cost lineEvery replicated byte is inter-region egress, priced per gigabyte, on top of the intra-cluster replication factor. Replicating a 20 MB/s topic across two regions is roughly 50 TB a month of egress before compression. Replicate selected topics, not everything, and make that an explicit decision per topic in the catalogue.
Keep the analytics path one-wayThe commonest legitimate multi-region flow is not active-active at all: every region replicates its events into one analytics cluster or lake, one direction only. No conflicts, no offset translation, no ordering requirement — and it satisfies most of what people ask multi-region for.
Residency in the topic designIf EU personal data may not leave the EU, that is a per-topic replication rule and an argument for keeping personal fields out of widely-replicated events. A thin event with an id, replicated globally, plus a fat event kept regional, is a common shape in regulated estates.
