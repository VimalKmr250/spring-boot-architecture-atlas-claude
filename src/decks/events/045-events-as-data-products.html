Events as data products

Once a topic has consumers outside your team, it stops being an implementation detail and becomes a product with users, a contract and a support obligation. Naming that explicitly is most of what "data mesh" means in practice.

What makes a topic a product

  • Discoverable — in the catalogue, with semantics, not just a schema.
  • Addressable — a stable name that survives your internal refactoring.
  • Trustworthy — a stated freshness SLO and completeness guarantee, monitored.
  • Self-describing — schema in a registry, field meanings documented, example payloads.
  • Governed — an owning team, access controls, a deprecation policy with notice periods.
  • Interoperable — shared conventions for ids, timestamps, money and the envelope.

None of that is technology. It is the operating model that stops a shared event platform degrading into a swamp of undocumented topics.

Two kinds of topic, treated differently

Internal
Inside one bounded context, one team both sides. Change freely, name it _internal.*, and make clear it is not a contract.
Published
Consumed outside the team. Versioned, documented, compatibility-gated, deprecation with notice. Changing it is a release, not a commit.

The failure is treating everything as the second — the ceremony makes internal work slow — or everything as the first, which breaks other teams weekly.

Do not publish your databaseCDC straight off your tables into a shared topic is the fastest way to give thirty teams a dependency on your schema. It is fine as an internal implementation of the outbox; it is not a data product. Publish domain events with business meaning, shaped deliberately — the anti-corruption layer applies here exactly as it does to a legacy integration.
