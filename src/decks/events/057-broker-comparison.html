Kafka, and when something else is better

Kafka is the default for enterprise event backbones and is the wrong choice more often than its popularity suggests. Honest comparison.

TechnologyModelStrong atWeak atPick it when
KafkaPartitioned, replayable logThroughput, replay, fan-out, stream processing, retention as a featurePer-message TTL, priority, complex routing, tiny deploymentsYou need a durable event backbone many consumers read independently
RabbitMQQueues with exchangesFlexible routing, per-message TTL, priorities, low latency, simple opsReplay (messages are consumed), very high throughputWork queues and RPC-style messaging with routing rules
IBM MQ / JMSQueues, transactionalXA transactions, guaranteed once-only delivery, decades of bank toolingFan-out, replay, throughput, costYou are integrating with systems that already speak it — extremely common in banking
AWS SQS + SNSManaged queue + pub/subZero operations, elastic, cheap at low volume, native DLQOrdering (FIFO queues are limited), replay, throughput ceilingsCloud-native workloads where you do not want to run a broker
EventBridge / Pub-SubManaged event busRouting rules, SaaS integrations, schema registry, no opsReplay, cost at high volume, vendor lock-inEvent routing across services and third parties, moderate volume
PulsarLog + queue, tiered by designMulti-tenancy, geo-replication, both queue and stream semanticsSmaller ecosystem, more moving parts (BookKeeper)Strong multi-tenancy or geo-replication requirements
The honest enterprise answerMost large organisations run two: Kafka as the event backbone for domain events and analytics, and an existing MQ estate for transactional integration with systems that will not change. That is not a failure to standardise — it is two different jobs. What matters is a clear rule for which goes where, written down.
Kafka's queue gap has closedKafka historically could not do per-message acknowledgement or competing consumers beyond the partition count. KIP-932 "queues for Kafka" adds share groups that acknowledge individual records and let consumer count exceed partition count. Early access in 4.0, preview in 4.1, GA in Kafka 4.2 behind the share.version feature flag (level 1 by default). On 4.2+ this is a real option for work-queue workloads instead of a second broker.
