Kafka is an append-only log, and everything follows from that

Not a queue. Records are not removed when read; consumers hold a position. That single difference is why replay, multiple independent consumers and stream processing are all possible.

Offsets are per group, per partition

Stored in the internal __consumer_offsets topic. Losing them means a group restarts from auto.offset.reset — earliest replays everything, latest silently skips the backlog.

Sequential IO is the speed

Appending to a file and sending bytes straight from page cache to socket is why a modest cluster handles hundreds of thousands of messages a second. Random access is what Kafka gives up in exchange.

Segments, not one file

Each partition is a series of segment files. Retention and compaction operate per segment, which is why deletion is cheap and why the active segment is never cleaned.
