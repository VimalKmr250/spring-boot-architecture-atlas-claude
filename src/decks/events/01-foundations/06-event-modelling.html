Getting the events right before getting Kafka right

Topics are cheap to create and expensive to change, because the moment someone consumes one you have a contract with a party you may not know. Spend the effort here.

Event storming, in a sentence

Put domain experts and engineers in a room. On orange notes, write everything that happens in the business, in past tense, left to right in time. Do not discuss systems.

Then cluster them. The clusters are your bounded contexts; the notes are your candidate events; the gaps are where nobody knows how the business actually works, which is the most valuable output of the day.

Half a day with the right six people routinely beats three weeks of design documents, because the disagreements surface in the room instead of in production.

What makes an event well-formed

  • Past tense, business language. OrderPlaced, not OrderTableInsert. If the business would not recognise the name, it is a database change notification wearing a costume.
  • One fact per event. OrderUpdated with a changeType field is four events pretending to be one, and every consumer starts with a switch statement.
  • Self-describing envelope. Event id, type, version, occurredAt, producer, correlation and causation ids.
  • Immutable. It records what happened. Corrections are new events, never edits.
  • Meaningful key. The entity it is about — that is what gives you ordering.
The envelope worth standardising organisation-wide
{
  "eventId":      "9c1f…",        // unique; consumers dedupe on this
  "eventType":    "OrderPlaced",
  "eventVersion": 2,
  "occurredAt":   "2026-09-16T01:12:04Z",   // business time
  "producer":     "order-platform",
  "correlationId":"req-4c9a…",    // the user action that started it all
  "causationId":  "evt-71bd…",    // the event that directly caused this one
  "tenantId":     "retailco-uk",
  "data":         { }             // the payload, versioned separately
