Topic naming, ownership and the catalogue

Once you pass twenty topics, the problem stops being technical. Nobody can tell what a topic contains, who owns it, whether it is safe to consume, or whether anyone still reads it.

A naming convention that answers the questions people ask
<domain>.<entity>.<event>.v<major>

orders.order.placed.v1
orders.order.cancelled.v1
payments.payment.authorised.v2
inventory.stock.reserved.v1

# Internal / intermediate topics, clearly marked as not-a-contract:
_internal.orders.dedupe.v1
orders.order.placed.v1.DLT
orders.order.placed.v1-retry-0

# Environment in the CLUSTER, never in the topic name — otherwise
# promotion between environments becomes a code change.
Why the major version is in the nameA breaking schema change becomes a new topic plus a dual-publish window, rather than a coordinated big-bang deploy across every consumer you cannot name. Consumers migrate on their own schedule; you retire v1 when the metrics say nobody reads it.

The event catalogue

One page per topic, generated where possible, holding: owning team, schema and its history, semantics in a sentence, the key, partition count, retention, expected volume, known consumers, and an example payload.

Without it, every integration starts with an engineer reading someone else's producer code to guess what a field means — and guessing wrong.

AsyncAPI is the OpenAPI equivalent for this, and a schema registry gives you the schema half for free. Generating the page from both keeps it true.

Ownership is the thing that rots firstA topic with no owning team is one nobody will change, fix or retire. Put the owner in the catalogue, in the topic's ACLs, and in the alert routing. When a team is disbanded, their topics need reassigning like any other asset.
Know who consumes youKafka does not tell the producer who reads a topic — that is the point, and it is also the problem when you want to change something. Monitor consumer groups per topic and publish the list. "Can I remove this field?" is then a query, not a broadcast email.
