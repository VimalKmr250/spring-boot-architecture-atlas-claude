Inside the producer: batching is the throughput

send() does not send. It appends to an in-memory batch for that partition; a background IO thread flushes batches to brokers. Understanding that one fact explains most producer tuning and most producer surprises.

The idempotent producer

Default-on as of Kafka 3.0 in name (KIP-679) — but a config-validation bug meant it did not actually engage until 3.0.1/3.1.1/3.2.0 (KAFKA-13598), so set it explicitly on anything older. Each producer gets a PID and a per-partition sequence number, so a broker can discard a duplicate caused by a retry. It prevents duplicates from retries within a session — not from your application sending twice.

send() is async — handle the failure

Fire-and-forget loses records silently. Either use the callback and log or route failures, or call get() and accept the throughput cost. In Spring, add a whenComplete to the returned future.

Ordering and in-flight requests

With idempotence on, up to 5 in-flight requests per connection still preserve order. With idempotence off, more than 1 in-flight plus retries can reorder records — a subtle way to lose per-key ordering.
