Rebalancing: the stop-the-world you can mostly avoid

When group membership changes, partitions are reassigned. Historically that meant every consumer stopped. Three mechanisms have made this far less painful, and most teams are still on the old defaults.

ProtocolBehaviourAvailability during rebalanceStatus
Eager (range / round-robin)Every consumer revokes all partitions, then reassignsTotal pause for the whole groupLegacy default in older clients
Cooperative stickyOnly the partitions that must move are revoked; the rest keep flowingMostly uninterruptedUse this on Kafka 3.x
KIP-848 consumer protocolAssignment computed by the group coordinator; no synchronisation barrier at allEffectively no pauseGA in Kafka 4.0 — the destination
Static membershipgroup.instance.id per pod: a restart within the timeout does not trigger a rebalanceNo rebalance on a rolling deployCombine with any of the above
Settings that stop rebalance storms
spring.kafka.consumer.properties:
  partition.assignment.strategy:
    org.apache.kafka.clients.consumer.CooperativeStickyAssignor
  group.instance.id: ${POD_NAME}        # static membership; unique and STABLE
  session.timeout.ms: 45000             # how long before a dead consumer is evicted
  heartbeat.interval.ms: 3000           # ~1/3 of session timeout
  max.poll.interval.ms: 300000          # max time BETWEEN polls — see the warning
  max.poll.records: 100                 # fewer records = shorter processing per poll

# On Kafka 4.x, prefer the new protocol instead of the assignor above:
#   group.protocol: consumer
The rebalance loop that looks like a broken consumerProcessing takes longer than max.poll.interval.ms → the broker assumes the consumer is dead → rebalance → the new owner reprocesses the same batch → also too slow → rebalance again. Lag climbs, nothing progresses, and the logs are full of rebalances rather than errors.

Fix by reducing max.poll.records so a poll's worth of work finishes quickly, not by raising the interval — raising it just delays detection of genuinely dead consumers.

Static membership needs a stable identitygroup.instance.id must be unique per consumer and the same across restarts — a StatefulSet pod name works, a random UUID defeats the purpose, and a duplicated value fences one of the two consumers out of the group entirely.
Commit before you lose the partitionImplement ConsumerRebalanceListener.onPartitionsRevoked (Spring exposes it on the container) to commit offsets for partitions you are about to hand over. Otherwise the new owner reprocesses everything since the last commit — correct, but wasteful and visible as duplicate side effects.
