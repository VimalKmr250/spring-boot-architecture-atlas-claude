Sizing and running the cluster

Enough to hold a credible conversation with your platform team, or to answer "how would you size this" without hand-waving.

Worked sizing

Volume
20,000 events/s × 1 KB = 20 MB/s of writes
Replication
× 3 = 60 MB/s written to disk across the cluster
Retention
7 days × 20 MB/s × 3 ≈ 36 TB of raw storage
Compression
lz4 at ~4× → ~9 TB actually on disk
Brokers
3 minimum for RF=3; 6 for headroom and faster recovery
Consumers
read amplification: five consumer groups means 5× the read traffic of the write traffic

Kafka is usually bound by network and disk throughput, rarely by CPU — unless you enable heavy compression on the broker or terminate TLS without offload.

KRaft: no more ZooKeeper

Kafka 4.0 removed ZooKeeper entirely. Metadata now lives in an internal Raft-replicated log managed by controller nodes, which means one system to run instead of two, much faster failover and controlled recovery, and far higher partition limits.

If you meet a cluster still on ZooKeeper, migration to KRaft is a prerequisite for any 4.x upgrade — and it is an operational project, not a config flag.

The operational essentials

  • Rack awareness so replicas of a partition land in different availability zones.
  • Quotas per client id so one badly-behaved producer cannot starve the cluster.
  • Cruise Control or equivalent for partition rebalancing when brokers are added.
  • Alert on under-replicated partitions, offline partitions, ISR shrink rate, disk usage, and request latency percentiles.
  • Rolling upgrades one broker at a time, waiting for URP to return to zero between each.
Managed or self-hostedRunning Kafka well is a full-time specialism. Unless you have a platform team that wants it, a managed service is usually the better economics — and in a bank the decision is often made for you by where the data may live.
