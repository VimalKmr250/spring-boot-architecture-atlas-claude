Partition count and the hot-partition problem

Partitions are your unit of parallelism, ordering and storage distribution. Two numbers to get right up front, because both are awkward to change later.

Choosing the count

Start from throughput, then take the maximum of the two bounds:

Target
peak 20,000 events/s on orders.placed
Consumer
one instance handles ~2,000/s → 10 partitions minimum
Headroom
3× for growth and for replaying a backlog fast → 24–32
Upper bound
partitions cost memory, file handles and leader-election time on the brokers. A few thousand per broker is fine; tens of thousands is not

Round to something you will not need to change. Adding partitions re-maps hash(key) % n, so a key's history and future land in different partitions and per-key ordering breaks across the change.

The clean way to repartition is a new topic at the new count, dual-publish, migrate consumers, retire the old one — the same expand/contract shape as a schema change.

Hot partitionsKey on customerId and your largest corporate account lands entirely on one partition. That consumer runs at 100% while five sit idle, and lag on that one partition is the lag your customers feel. Kafka will not rebalance around it, because the key contract forbids moving the data.

Detect it by graphing lag per partition, not lag per topic — the topic average hides a single hot partition completely.

Fixes for a hot key

  • Key more finely — orderId instead of customerId, if per-customer ordering is not actually required.
  • Composite key with a salt — customerId:bucket, where the bucket is derived from the order id. Spreads load, preserves nothing about customer ordering, so check that is acceptable.
  • Separate topic for the handful of enormous accounts, with its own consumer group and its own scaling.
  • Custom partitioner for genuinely skewed distributions — powerful, and an obligation to maintain forever.
