You will ship your org chart — plan for it

Conway's law is not a warning, it is a design tool. Pick the team structure you want the architecture to have, and the architecture follows within two quarters.

Team → module map

Checkout
cart, pricing, order — owns the customer-facing latency budget
Payments
payment module + gateway adapters; PCI scope lives here alone
Supply
inventory, fulfilment; owns ERP and WMS integrations
Catalog
catalog, search projection
Platform
no business module; owns gateway, observability, CI, shared libs
Rule of thumbOne module has exactly one owning team. A team may own several modules; a module owned by two teams has no owner, and its boundary will erode within months.
Watch forAn "integration team" or a "database team" is an inverse Conway smell: it means the real boundaries run through the middle of your modules and every change needs a hand-off.
Cognitive load, not headcountThe useful limit on a module is how much a team can hold in their heads: one domain language, one data model, one set of external systems. When a team needs two glossaries to explain their module, split it.
