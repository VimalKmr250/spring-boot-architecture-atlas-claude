DR: two numbers the business owns, one drill you must run

RTO and RPO are business decisions with engineering price tags. The engineering failure is not choosing wrong numbers — it is having numbers nobody has ever tested.

The conversation to have with the business

Not "what RTO do you want" — the answer is always zero. Instead: "one hour of downtime costs roughly £X in lost orders and £Y in remediation. Going from a 4-hour RTO to a 15-minute RTO costs £Z a year. Which do you want?"

That question gets a real answer, gets it signed off, and gives you something to point at when the budget is reviewed. It is also exactly the reasoning an interviewer is listening for.

Untested is untrueA DR plan that has never been executed is a document, not a capability. The common discoveries on a first real drill: the restore takes nine hours, not one; a schema was never being backed up; the runbook references a person who left; the standby's credentials expired six months ago.
Make it routineQuarterly, in business hours, with a rollback plan: fail over to the standby, run for an hour, fail back. Publish the measured RTO. Teams that do this have a boring DR story; teams that do not have an exciting one exactly once.
Do not forget the dependenciesYour service failing over is pointless if the identity provider, the secret store or the payment gateway is single-region. DR is a property of the whole path, and the weakest link sets the real RTO.
