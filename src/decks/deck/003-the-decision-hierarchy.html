Six decisions, in this order

Most architecture arguments are people debating level 4 while disagreeing about level 1. Decide top-down; each level constrains the one below it, and reversing a level costs roughly ten times what the level below costs.

#DecisionOur answer for the order platformCost to reverse
1Deployment styleModular monolith, one deployable, extract on evidencemonths
2Module boundariesEight bounded contexts from the domain, not from the org chartweeks–months
3Inter-module contractPublished API for queries, domain events for reactionsweeks
4Data ownershipSchema per module in one PostgreSQL cluster, no cross-schema joinsweeks
5Internal layeringPorts and adapters inside each moduledays
6Frameworks & librariesSpring Boot 4.1, JPA + JdbcClient, Kafka, Angular SPA + BFFdays
Common failureTeams start at level 6 ("we'll use Kafka and Kubernetes"), then discover at level 2 that their modules share a customer table and cannot be separated at all. Infrastructure never fixes a boundary problem.
Why order mattersLevels 1–4 are architecture: expensive, slow, political. Levels 5–6 are design: a competent team changes them in a sprint. Spend your review time at the top.
