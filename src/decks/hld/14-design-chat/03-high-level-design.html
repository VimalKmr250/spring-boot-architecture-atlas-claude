Stateful gateways hold connections; stateless services decide what to deliver where

Separate the parts that must be stateful from those that needn't be. Gateways hold hundreds of thousands of WebSockets each and do almost nothing else. The chat service persists messages and assigns sequence numbers. Delivery workers look up where each recipient is connected, route the message there, and fall back to a push notification when the recipient is offline.

Why stateful gateways stay thin

Gateways deploy slowly: every restart drops half a million sockets, and those clients must reconnect. So keep business logic out of them. They authenticate the socket, register it, relay frames, and push what delivery workers hand them.

Routing to a connection

The session registry maps user → {gateway, device}. Workers forward to that gateway over RPC, or to a per-gateway pub/sub channel. Entries have a TTL refreshed by heartbeats, so a crashed gateway's entries expire on their own.

Sync covers every gap

Real-time delivery is an optimisation. The source of truth is "messages with seq greater than my last acknowledged one". On every reconnect, each device asks for what it missed, which also repairs any delivery that was dropped.
