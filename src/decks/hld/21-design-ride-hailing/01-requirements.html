Design ride-hailing: a firehose of moving points, and a trip record that must never double-book

Riders request a trip, the system finds a nearby driver, both follow the trip live on a map, and the rider pays at the end. Two very different workloads hide in that sentence. Driver locations are a huge, ephemeral write stream where only the latest point matters. The trip itself is low-volume state that must be strongly consistent: one driver per trip, one trip per driver.

Functional

  • Riders see nearby cars and an ETA, then request a ride.
  • Match to a driver, who accepts or declines within seconds.
  • Live tracking during pickup and trip; fare and payment at the end.

Non-functional

  • Match within a few seconds; locations at most a few seconds stale.
  • Never assign one driver to two trips, or one trip to two drivers.
  • Highly available per city: an outage in one city mustn't spread.

Clarify

  • Ride types (standard, XL)? Shared rides (pooling) are a follow-up.
  • Surge pricing in scope? Payments through a provider?
  • How many cities and regions? Is data residency required?
