Deep dive 2: rank by road ETA, offer one driver at a time, and let a guarded write settle races

The closest driver in a straight line is often not the fastest to arrive, because of rivers, one-way streets and highways. Matching ranks candidates by ETA from a routing engine, then offers the trip to the best one under a short lock. The trip itself is a state machine whose transitions are conditional updates, so two drivers accepting at the same instant can never both win.

One offer at a time, or a few?

Offering to one driver at a time is fair and simple but adds up to 10 s per decline. Offering to the top 2–3 at once is faster, but all but one of them see "taken", which frustrates drivers. Many systems offer sequentially with short timeouts, and widen when demand is high.

Batch matching

Greedy matching (each request takes its best driver immediately) is locally optimal but globally poor. Collecting requests for 1–2 s and solving an assignment over the batch, minimising total ETA, noticeably cuts average pickup time in busy areas.

Idempotent everything

Ride creation uses an idempotency key, so a double tap makes one ride. Accepts are keyed by offer id, so a retried accept is a no-op. Driver status (free or on trip) is derived from the trip row, not maintained as a separate flag that can drift.
