A location pipeline and a ride pipeline that meet only at the geo index

Drivers hold persistent connections to a gateway fleet. Their locations update an in-memory geo index and flow on to a stream for everything else: ETAs, surge, trip tracking and history. Ride requests go through a matching service that queries the index, ranks candidates by ETA and pushes an offer down the driver's open connection. The trip service records the outcome with a guarded write.

The gateway fleet is stateful

Millions of long-lived sockets need a connection registry (driver → gateway node) so matching can push an offer to the right node. It's the same pattern as chat presence. When a gateway node dies, its drivers reconnect elsewhere and re-register within seconds.

Why the stream matters

One point feeds many consumers: the rider's live map during the trip, ETA models, surge computation per cell, fraud checks for teleporting phones, and the fare's distance. A topic keyed by driver id lets each consume independently and keeps per-driver order.

Rider updates by push

While matching and during the trip, the rider's app receives status changes and the driver's position (every few seconds) over its own socket or push channel. Don't poll GET /rides, which multiplies reads for no benefit.
