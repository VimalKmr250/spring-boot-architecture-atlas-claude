Deep dive 1: turn "drivers near this point" into "drivers in these few cells"

A database scan over latitude and longitude doesn't scale to a million moving points. Instead, divide the map into cells, keep a set of driver ids per cell, and answer "nearby" by reading the rider's cell and its neighbours. The three common cell schemes trade simplicity, adaptiveness and uniform distances.

Redis GEO, or your own index

GEOADD and GEOSEARCH store points in a sorted set scored by a 52-bit geohash. That's simple and fast per city. At larger scale, matching services keep an in-process map of cell → drivers for their shard of cells, fed directly from the location stream.

250 k writes/s is mostly overwrites

A driver changes cell only every few updates. Update the position in place, and move the id between cell sets only when the cell changes. Shard by city, then by cell range within big cities, so no single node sees more than tens of thousands of writes a second.

"Cars near me" is a cache

Riders browsing the map don't need an exact live query. Publish a per-cell snapshot every 2–5 s, including jittered positions for driver privacy, and serve it from a cache. A million reads a second become cheap cache hits.
