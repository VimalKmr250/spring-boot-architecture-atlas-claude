Cities are natural cells, and surge pricing is a control loop you must damp

Almost every ride starts and ends in one metro area. That makes the city the natural unit of sharding and of failure: each city (or cluster of small cities) runs its own location, matching and trip stack, and only accounts, payments and analytics are global. The other thing interviewers probe is surge pricing, a feedback loop between price, demand and supply that oscillates if nobody damps it.

Follow-upAnswer sketch
Shared ridesMatching becomes route insertion: add a rider to a moving trip only if every passenger's detour stays under a limit.
Accurate ETAsA routing engine over a road graph with live traffic (contraction hierarchies for speed), corrected by ML trained on past trips.
GPS spoofingReject impossible speeds and teleports, cross-check with network location, and use device attestation.
PaymentAuthorise an estimate at request time, capture the final fare at completion; tips are a separate later capture.
Summarise"Drivers stream locations over sockets into a per-city in-memory cell index and a Kafka stream. Matching reads nearby cells, ranks candidates by road ETA, and offers under a short lock. The trip is a state machine whose assignment is a guarded write with one active trip per driver. Cities are cells, so failures stay local, and surge is a damped loop fed by the streams."
