Deep dive 2: from 1,500 candidates to 20 ranked posts in under 200 ms

A ranked feed is a funnel. Gather candidates cheaply, filter out what must never show, score what's left with a model, then fetch full content only for the handful you'll return. Each stage cuts the set by an order of magnitude, so the expensive stages see little data.

Chronological is a special case

Score = timestamp and skip ③ and ④. Designing the funnel anyway means switching between "latest" and "top" is a flag rather than a rewrite, and ads or recommendations slot in as just another candidate source.

Features must be precomputed

The ranker can't query ten services per candidate. Author–viewer affinity, post engagement velocity and user embeddings are computed by streaming jobs into a feature store, and read in batches (< 10 ms) at ranking time.

Stable pagination

Rank the first page and cache the ordered candidate list for the session (say 10 minutes), so page two continues that snapshot. Re-ranking on every page would reshuffle items: duplicates, and posts that vanish between scrolls.
