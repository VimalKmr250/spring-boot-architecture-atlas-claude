Consensus lets a cluster agree on one leader and one log, as long as a majority is up

Many designs need exactly one of something: one primary database, one scheduler, one lock holder. Deciding who, safely, while nodes crash and messages go missing, is the consensus problem. Raft and Paxos solve it with majorities. In practice you rarely implement them yourself. You use etcd, ZooKeeper or Consul, or a database that runs Raft internally.

Why odd cluster sizes

A majority of 3 is 2, so 3 nodes tolerate 1 failure. A majority of 5 is 3, so 5 nodes tolerate 2. Four nodes also tolerate only 1, while paying more latency per write. Consensus clusters are small (3 or 5 nodes) and kept for metadata, not bulk data.

What to use it for

Leader election (one scheduler, one primary), distributed locks with leases, service registries, cluster membership, configuration, and the metadata of bigger systems. Kafka's KRaft, CockroachDB ranges and Kubernetes' etcd all sit on it.

Its costs

Every write is a round trip to a majority, so cross-region consensus costs cross-region latency. A minority partition can't make progress; that is the CP choice from §02. Leases depend on clocks being roughly right, which is why fencing tokens exist.
