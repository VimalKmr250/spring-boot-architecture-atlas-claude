Partition resources so one slow dependency can't drain them all

Named after a ship's watertight compartments: a bulkhead gives each dependency or workload its own bounded pool of threads, connections or instances, so a leak in one can't sink the ship. Load shedding is its partner at the front door. When the whole service is saturated, reject the least important requests early, so the important ones still get through.

Kinds of bulkhead

A thread or connection pool per dependency. Separate deployments for API, workers and admin traffic. Separate clusters for your largest tenants (cells, §10). Per-tenant rate limits. The same principle applies at every scale: bound the blast radius.

Sizing a pool

Use Little's law: pool = peak rate × p99 latency × headroom. At 100 req/s to a dependency with a 200 ms p99, that's about 20 slots, so give it 30. A pool that is too big stops being a bulkhead; one that is too small throttles healthy traffic.

Shedding signals

Shed on concurrency or queueing delay (in-flight requests, time spent in queue), not only CPU. Prioritise by request class, carried in a header. Prefer retrying clients' second attempts last. Return 503 with Retry-After so well-behaved clients back off.
