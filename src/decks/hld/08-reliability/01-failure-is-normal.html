At scale, something is always broken, so design for partial failure

With 1,000 servers that each fail once every three years, you lose about one a day. Networks drop, delay and reorder packets. A dependency can be up but ten times slower than usual. Reliability doesn't mean preventing failure. It means containing it: one component's failure should degrade one feature, not take down the system.

Timeouts on every remote call

No call waits forever. Set timeouts from the dependency's p99.9 and the caller's own deadline, not from library defaults, some of which are infinite. A missing timeout is how one slow service cascades into a full outage.

Redundancy, and no shared fate

At least two of everything, across failure domains (hosts, zones, regions). Watch for hidden shared fate: the same config push, deploy, certificate, DNS provider or quota can take down all the replicas at once.

Degrade gracefully

Decide in advance what each feature does when a dependency is down. Recommendations disappear, prices come from a cache, reviews show "unavailable", and checkout still works. Write it down as a dependency matrix and test it.
