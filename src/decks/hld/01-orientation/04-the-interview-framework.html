Eight steps in 45 minutes, and you drive

A system-design round is scored on how you get to an answer, not on reaching one particular diagram. The eight steps make your reasoning visible and keep you from spending 30 minutes on the database schema. Every worked design in this deck follows them in order.

StepWhat you doSay out loud, e.g.
1 · ClarifyFunctional scope (3–5 core features), non-functional targets (scale, latency, availability, consistency), what is out of scope"I'll focus on create and redirect. Analytics can be eventually consistent. Is that fair?"
2 · EstimateDAU → QPS, peak, read:write ratio, storage over 5 years, bandwidth, cache size"100 M writes a day is about 1,200 a second, so one Postgres primary can take it."
3 · APIThe 3–5 endpoints or messages that serve the core features, including idempotency and paging"POST /v1/urls takes an idempotency key, so a retried create can't make two links."
4 · Data modelEntities, key fields, access patterns, then SQL or NoSQL and the partition key"Every read is by short code, so this is a key-value access pattern."
5 · High-level designClients → edge → services → stores → async consumers. Walk one request end to end."A redirect never touches the write path. Here's the read path…"
6 · Deep divesThe two hardest parts: usually the core algorithm and the scaling bottleneck"The interesting problems are generating unique keys and keeping redirects under 10 ms."
7 · BottlenecksHot spots, single points of failure, what breaks at 10×, the failure modes of each dependency"If Redis dies, redirects fall back to the read replicas, which are sized to carry the full read load."
8 · Wrap-upSummarise the trade-offs you made and what you would do next"I chose availability over consistency for reads. Next I'd add multi-region."
What is actually scoredFour things: navigating the problem (did you find the hard part?), the design (does it work end to end?), trade-offs (did you name the alternatives and why you rejected them?), and communication (could the interviewer follow you?). Senior candidates are also expected to raise failure modes and operability without being asked.
The classic ways to loseDesigning before clarifying. Drawing boxes with no data flow. Naming technologies without reasons ("Kafka, because it scales"). Going deep too early. Waiting for the interviewer to drive. And never mentioning what happens when something fails.
