Limit at the edge in layers: coarse per IP first, precise per key at the gateway

Rejecting a request is cheapest as early as possible. The CDN or WAF drops floods by IP before they reach you. The gateway enforces precise per-key and per-endpoint limits against a shared counter store. Services keep a last-line concurrency limit for their own expensive work.

Why at the gateway, not in each service

One place to enforce and observe limits. The check happens before expensive work (auth, fan-out) and uses the authenticated identity. Services stay free of limit logic. The trade-off is that the gateway becomes critical, so the filter must be fast and have a failure mode.

Why a shared store and not local counters

With 50 gateways and a load balancer spreading requests, local counters each see about 1/50 of a key's traffic, so per-instance limits are wildly inaccurate for bursty clients. A shared atomic counter makes the limit exact, at the cost of about 0.5 ms and a dependency. Deep dive 2 shows the hybrids.
