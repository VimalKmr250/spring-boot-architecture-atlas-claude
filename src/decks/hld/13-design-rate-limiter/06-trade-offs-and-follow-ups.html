Limits are product decisions as well as defences

A limiter that works technically can still fail its users. A partner who gets throttled with no warning, fair customers starved by one heavy customer, a limit change that locks out your biggest account. Wrap up with the operational and product sides, and with how the design grows.

RiskMitigation
Redis hot shard (one huge client)Shard that client's key with sub-keys, or give it a lease-based local budget
Limit change locks out a big customerShadow mode first, gradual rollout, per-customer overrides
Clients hammer after 429Retry-After, SDKs with backoff + jitter, stricter limits on repeated violators
Limiter adds latencyKeep Redis local to the region, pipeline multi-rule checks, lease tokens

Follow-ups to expect

  • "Limit concurrent requests, not rate?" A semaphore per key: increment on start, decrement on finish, with a TTL for crashed requests.
  • "Daily quotas for billing?" A separate durable counter, since Redis alone isn't billing-grade. Reconcile from the decision stream.
  • "Distributed DDoS?" That's the edge/WAF layer: anycast, IP reputation, challenges. Not this service.
