Five numbers, derived the same way every time

Estimation isn't about being right. It tells you which parts of the design matter. Two minutes of arithmetic answers questions like "does this fit on one database?", "do we need a CDN?" and "can the cache hold the hot set?". Say the assumptions out loud, round hard, and write the results where you can point at them later.

Round aggressively

86,400 becomes 10⁵. 365 becomes 400 when you are rounding up. 2.3 becomes 2. Interviewers want the order of magnitude and a clean chain of reasoning, not long division. State "about" and move on.

Compare to one box

Every number means something only next to a capacity. 3,000 writes/s fits one PostgreSQL primary, and 300,000 does not. 2 TB fits one node, and 2 PB needs object storage. That comparison is the reason for doing the estimate.

Separate the hard dimension

Usually only one number is scary: the QPS (feeds), the storage (video), the fan-out (notifications), or the concurrency (chat). Name it. It becomes your first deep dive.
