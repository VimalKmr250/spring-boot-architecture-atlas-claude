Worked estimate: a photo-sharing app with 100 million daily users

The assumptions, said out loud: 100 M daily active users. Each user views about 50 photos a day, and one user in five uploads one photo a day. An original photo compresses to about 500 KB, and its thumbnails add about 100 KB. A feed-sized image served to a phone is about 100 KB. Peak traffic is 3× the average.

The script, spoken

"100 million users times 50 views is 5 billion a day. Divided by about 10⁵ seconds, that's roughly 50 thousand a second, and about 150 thousand at peak. Each view is about 100 KB, so tens of gigabits per second leave the system. That has to come from a CDN. Uploads are 20 million a day, a few hundred a second, but at 600 KB each that's 12 terabytes a day. So photos go to object storage, and only metadata goes to a database."

Sanity checks worth a sentence

  • Is 50 views a day plausible? It is for a scrolling feed. For a utility app, it might be 5.
  • Storage is often dominated by derived data: thumbnails, transcodes, indexes, replicas. Add it.
  • Retention changes everything: keep forever, or delete after 30 days?
  • Erasure coding (≈ 1.5×) instead of 3 full replicas saves a third of the raw storage at PB scale.
