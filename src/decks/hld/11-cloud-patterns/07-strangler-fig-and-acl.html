Replace a legacy system one route at a time, behind a facade

Big-bang rewrites of systems with years of encoded business rules fail often and expensively. The strangler fig puts a routing facade in front of the legacy system, moves one capability at a time to new services, and retires the old system when nothing routes to it. An anti-corruption layer translates between the new model and the legacy one, so legacy concepts don't leak into the new code.

Choosing what to move first

Pick a capability that has a clear boundary, real business value and a pain point: it changes often, scales badly or breaks often. Avoid the most entangled core first. Use tracing to see which routes carry traffic and which tables each one touches.

The data is the hard part

Routes move easily, data doesn't. Common approaches: the new service reads legacy tables through an ACL; CDC syncs legacy tables into the new store; dual-write for a transition window with reconciliation. Cut over ownership last, once the reconciliation reports are clean.

Anti-corruption layer

A translator (an adapter, a small service, or an event mapper) that converts legacy formats, codes and concepts into the new domain's language, and back. It keeps "CUST_TYP_CD = 3" out of the new code, and it's also how you integrate third-party systems you'll never control.
