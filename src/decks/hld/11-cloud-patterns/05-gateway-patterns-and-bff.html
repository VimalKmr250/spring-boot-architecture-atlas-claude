One front door: route, aggregate, offload, and shape responses per client

The gateway patterns are three jobs a front door can do, and they are often combined. Routing hides many services behind one endpoint. Aggregation turns several backend calls into one client call. Offloading centralises cross-cutting work such as TLS and auth. A backend for frontend applies all three, separately for each client type.

Keep the gateway thinAn aggregating gateway is on every request's critical path and is shared by every team. Put in it only generic, declarative concerns and simple composition. When composition needs business rules, it belongs in a BFF owned by the client team, or in a proper service. A gateway also needs timeouts per downstream call and partial-result fallbacks, or a slow reviews service makes the whole product page slow.
