An SLO turns "highly available" into a number you can spend

An SLI is a measurement, e.g. the share of requests served successfully within 300 ms. An SLO is the target for it: 99.9% over 30 days. An SLA is the contract with a penalty, set looser than the SLO so you breach the SLO internally before you owe anyone money. The gap between 100% and the SLO is the error budget.

Good SLIs are user-shaped

Measure at the edge, where the user feels it: the success ratio and latency of real requests to a journey ("place order", "load feed"). CPU and memory are causes, not SLIs.

Pick targets you can defend

A target stricter than your dependencies is fiction: you can't be 99.99% on top of a 99.9% database without redundancy. Start from what users tolerate and what you achieve today, then tighten.

Budgets make trade-offs explicit

Budget left over means you can ship faster and run experiments. Budget exhausted means reliability comes first. Product and engineering argue about data instead of opinions.
