Partitions are rare; the latency-versus-consistency trade-off is paid on every request

CAP only describes behaviour during a partition. PACELC adds the everyday case: if Partitioned, choose A or C; Else, choose Latency or Consistency. Waiting for replicas to confirm a write makes it consistent, and it makes the request slower. That trade-off applies on every request, not only during a partition.

PA/EL fast and always up

Cassandra, DynamoDB (default reads), Riak. Writes are acknowledged by one or a few replicas and reads may be stale. Good for feeds, carts, sensor data, and anything that has to be write-heavy and low-latency.

PC/EC correct, and pays for it

Spanner, HBase, etcd / ZooKeeper, VoltDB. Writes wait for a quorum or synchronous replicas. Good for money, metadata, coordination and schedules. Expect cross-zone round-trips on each write.

PA/EC consistent until it can't be

MongoDB, in Abadi's classification: it reads from the primary in normal operation, but during some partitions can serve writes that are later rolled back.

PC/EL fast normally, strict under stress

Yahoo PNUTS, the classic example: it serves reads from the local replica for low latency, but refuses writes to a record whose master is unreachable.

The number to rememberSynchronous replication between availability zones in one region adds roughly 1–2 ms per write, which is usually worth it. Synchronous replication across continents adds 60–150 ms per write, which is why global systems are nearly always EL across regions and EC within one.
