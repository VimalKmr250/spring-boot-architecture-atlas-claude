Availability multiplies in series and compounds in parallel

Availability is the fraction of time, or of requests, that succeed. Each nine you add cuts the allowed downtime by 10×, and the cost of the system goes up much more steeply than that. Two rules of arithmetic decide most availability arguments in an interview.

MTBF, MTTR, and where the nines come from

Availability = MTBF ÷ (MTBF + MTTR). You can fail less often (raise the mean time between failures) or recover faster (cut the mean time to recovery). Past three nines, the cheaper lever is usually recovery: automated fail-over, fast rollback, feature flags, and good alerts. At 99.99% there is no time for a human to wake up and log in.

Say it this way"The checkout path has four synchronous dependencies. At 99.9% each, that's about 99.6%. To reach 99.95% I'd make recommendations and loyalty asynchronous and give tax calculation a cached fallback. That leaves two dependencies on the critical path."
