Latency is how long one request takes; throughput is how many finish per second

Think of a pipe. Its length is the latency and its width is the throughput. You can widen the pipe (more workers, more partitions) without shortening it, and a pipe running nearly full gets slower for everyone, because requests start queueing. Aim for the most throughput you can get while latency stays acceptable.

Report percentiles, never the mean

A mean of 40 ms can hide a p99 of 2 s. Users experience the tail, and your heaviest users hit it most often. Set targets as p50 / p99 (and p99.9 for critical paths).

Fan-out amplifies the tail

If one request calls 100 backends and each is slow 1% of the time, then 1 − 0.99100 ≈ 63% of requests are slow. Mitigations: hedged requests, tighter per-call timeouts, and returning partial results.

Trading one for the other

Batching raises throughput and adds latency (Kafka's linger.ms, group commit). Parallelism lowers latency and costs throughput headroom. Say which one the requirement values.
