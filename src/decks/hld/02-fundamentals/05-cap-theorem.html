During a network partition, choose: refuse to answer, or answer possibly wrong

CAP says a distributed data store cannot be both Consistent (every read sees the latest write, i.e. linearizable) and Available (every request to a live node gets a non-error answer) while a Partition splits the network. Networks do partition, so P is not optional. The real choice is what each node does when it can't reach the others.

CP: correctness over uptime

Use it where a wrong answer costs money or trust: balances, inventory counts at checkout, seat and room bookings, leader election, locks, configuration. etcd ZooKeeper Spanner HBase MongoDB (majority)

AP: uptime over freshness

Use it where stale is fine and down is not: timelines, likes, product views, shopping carts, presence, metrics. You need conflict resolution: last-write-wins, CRDTs, or merging in the application. Cassandra DynamoDB Riak CouchDB

The mature answerChoose per operation, not per system. An e-commerce site can be AP for browsing and the cart, and CP for "decrement stock and take payment". Many stores let you tune this per request, for example Cassandra's QUORUM vs ONE, or DynamoDB's strongly consistent reads.
