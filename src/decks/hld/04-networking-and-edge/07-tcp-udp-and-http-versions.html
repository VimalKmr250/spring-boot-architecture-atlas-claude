Every new connection costs round trips, so reuse them

TCP guarantees ordered, reliable delivery and pays for it with handshakes and head-of-line blocking. UDP guarantees nothing and adds nothing. HTTP/3 runs over QUIC, which rebuilds TCP's reliability on top of UDP with fewer round trips and independent streams. For system design, the lesson is: long-lived, pooled connections, and fewer round trips on the critical path.

What this means for designsPool connections to databases and services, because a new TLS connection per call adds 2–3 RTT. Keep chatty protocols inside a datacenter. For real-time media, prefer UDP-based transports (WebRTC), where a late packet is worthless and should be dropped, not retransmitted.
