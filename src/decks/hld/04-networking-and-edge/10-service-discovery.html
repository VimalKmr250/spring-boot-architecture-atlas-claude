Instances come and go, so callers look them up rather than hard-coding them

With autoscaling and rolling deploys, the set of healthy instances behind a service changes by the minute. Service discovery is a registry of what's healthy right now, plus a way for callers to use it: either the client asks the registry and picks an instance, or a load balancer does that on the client's behalf.

Health decides membership

An instance should enter the registry only when it is ready (warmed caches, connections open) and leave the moment it starts draining. Liveness ("restart me") and readiness ("send me traffic") are different probes. Mixing them up causes restart loops.

The registry is critical infrastructure

If the registry goes down, nothing can find anything. Registries are replicated consensus clusters (etcd, Consul). Clients cache the last known list, so a registry outage freezes membership instead of breaking calls.

DNS-based discovery caveats

DNS is universal, but clients cache answers, sometimes past the TTL (the JVM's own DNS cache is a classic example), and DNS carries no load information. That is fine for coarse routing. Use a proxy or a client-side balancer for per-request balancing.
