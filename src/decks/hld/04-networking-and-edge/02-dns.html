DNS turns names into addresses, and it is also your first load balancer

A lookup walks a hierarchy from the root to the top-level domain to the domain's own nameserver, and every answer is cached for its TTL. That caching makes DNS fast and massively scalable. It is also why a DNS change or DNS fail-over is never instant.

Records you'll mention

A/AAAA name → IPv4/IPv6. CNAME is an alias to another name (how you point at a CDN or load balancer). NS delegates to nameservers. MX is mail, TXT holds verification and SPF, and SRV gives a service's host and port.

DNS as traffic management

Managed DNS can answer differently per query. Weighted answers split traffic (canary 5%). Latency or geo answers send users to the nearest region. Fail-over answers stop returning an unhealthy region. That makes it the usual way to route between regions.

TTL is a trade-off

A short TTL (30–60 s) means faster fail-over and more lookups. A long TTL (hours) means fewer lookups and slow changes. Some clients and ISPs ignore TTLs, so plan for a stale tail of traffic after any change, and lower the TTL a day before a planned migration.
