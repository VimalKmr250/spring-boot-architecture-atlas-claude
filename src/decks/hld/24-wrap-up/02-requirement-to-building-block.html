Read the requirement, reach for the block: the map behind all twelve designs

After a dozen designs a pattern shows. Each requirement phrase triggers a small set of building blocks, and the design is mostly a matter of composing them and justifying each one. Use this map to generate candidates fast. Then make the trade-off explicit before committing.

Start from the verbs

"Upload", "notify", "search", "match", "pay": each verb in the requirements usually becomes one service or one pipeline. Group verbs that share data and consistency needs, and split those that don't.

Then from the numbers

The estimate tells you which blocks are mandatory. Tens of Tbps of egress means a CDN. 250 k writes/s means an in-memory or LSM store. A 100:1 read ratio means a cache. The numbers also rule blocks out.

Then from the failures

For each block, ask what happens when it's slow, down or duplicated. The answers add the timeouts, retries with idempotency, queues, fallbacks and reconciliation jobs that separate a senior answer from a diagram.
