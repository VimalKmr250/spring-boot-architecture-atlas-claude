Four weeks from vocabulary to timed mocks, and where to go deeper

Reading slides builds recognition, but interviews test recall under time pressure. The plan below alternates learning a section with producing something from memory, such as a diagram, an estimate or a spoken trade-off, and finishes with timed runs of the twelve designs. The reading list is short on purpose: a few sources, read properly.

WeekStudyPractise from memory
1Orientation, Fundamentals, Estimation, Networking & edgeOne estimate a day, out loud and in under 5 minutes. Explain CAP vs PACELC and L4 vs L7 to a rubber duck.
2Data, Caching, AsynchronismRedraw the hash ring, leader-follower lag, cache-aside and the outbox from a blank page. Pick a store for five made-up products.
3Reliability, Operate & secure, Architecture and cloud patternsFor three designs, walk every hop's failure: timeout, retry, fallback. Write SLOs and one burn-rate alert for each.
4The twelve designsTwo a day, 45 minutes each on a whiteboard, then compare with the slides. Finish with at least two mock interviews with a peer.

Books

DDIA
Kleppmann, Designing Data-Intensive Applications: the foundation for replication, partitioning, transactions and streams.
SDI 1 & 2
Alex Xu, System Design Interview: many more worked designs in interview format.
SRE
Google, Site Reliability Engineering: SLOs, alerting, overload and incident practice.
DB internals
Petrov, Database Internals: B-trees, LSM trees and distributed consensus in depth.

roadmap.sh/system-design, the path this deck follows.

Builders' Library
AWS Builders' Library: timeouts, retries, shuffle sharding and load shedding from practice.

Papers worth an evening

  • Dynamo (2007): the KV store design, from the source.
  • Bigtable (2006) and GFS (2003): LSM and storage at scale.
  • Kafka (2011): the log as a building block.
  • Raft (2014): consensus you can explain.
  • Spanner (2012): global consistency with TrueTime.
  • The Tail at Scale (2013): why p99 dominates fan-out.
