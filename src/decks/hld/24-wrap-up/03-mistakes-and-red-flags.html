What sinks a system-design round, and what to say instead

Most failed rounds don't fail on missing knowledge. They fail on process: skipping requirements, hiding the numbers, naming products instead of reasoning, or never discussing what breaks. Each row pairs a red flag interviewers write down with the behaviour that earns the opposite note.

Red flagWhy it hurtsDo this instead
Drawing boxes in minute oneYou design the wrong system, confidently.Spend five minutes on functional and non-functional requirements; write them down and confirm.
No numbersEvery later choice is unjustified: why a cache, why sharding?Estimate QPS, storage and bandwidth; circle the one number that shapes the design.
Buzzword soup ("add Kafka, Redis, Kubernetes")Products without reasons signal memorisation.Name the property you need (durable ordered log), then the product that has it.
One database does everythingIgnores access patterns and the read/write mix.Pick storage per access pattern and say what each one is the source of truth for.
Happy path onlyReal systems are mostly failure handling.For each hop: timeout, retry with idempotency, fallback, and what the user sees.
"Exactly-once" by assertionHand-waves the hardest part of distributed systems.At-least-once delivery plus idempotent consumers, and say where the dedupe key lives.
Over-engineering day oneMicroservices and multi-region for 1,000 users.Design for 10× the stated load, then describe what changes at 100×.
Silent choicesThe interviewer can't score reasoning they can't hear.State the trade-off for every major decision (see the cheat sheet).
Losing the clockNo deep dives means no senior signal.Keep the HLD simple enough to finish by minute 25, then go deep where the risk is.
Defending instead of adaptingNew constraints are a test of flexibility."Good point: that breaks X, so I'd change Y." Treat hints as gifts.
Sounds weak"We'll just use NoSQL because it scales, and put a cache in front of everything. For consistency we'll use transactions."
Sounds senior"Reads are 100× writes and a few seconds of staleness is fine, so a cache-aside layer with a 60 s TTL takes most of the load. Orders need atomic stock checks, so those go through a guarded update in PostgreSQL."
