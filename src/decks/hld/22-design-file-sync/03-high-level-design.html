Bytes through a block service, truth through a metadata service, and a poke to everyone else

The client does much of the work: it watches the folder, chunks and hashes files, and keeps a local database of what it believes is synced. On the server, the block service stores immutable blocks in blob storage and the metadata service commits journal entries. A notification service tells other devices that a namespace changed. They then pull the changes and fetch any blocks they lack.

The client is half the system

The watcher debounces bursts of events (an editor saving through a temp file and a rename). The local state database remembers the last synced revision and hash per path. That is how the client tells "I changed this" from "the server changed this", and how it avoids re-uploading after a restart.

Notifications are cheap to hold

Hundreds of millions of devices keep one long-poll or WebSocket open to the notification service, not to the metadata service. A commit publishes to a pub/sub channel per namespace, and only the notification servers holding subscribers wake up.

Blocks and metadata scale apart

Blob storage grows with bytes and is optimised for throughput and durability. The metadata tier grows with operations and is optimised for transactional commits. Different teams can scale, tune and fail over each one independently.
