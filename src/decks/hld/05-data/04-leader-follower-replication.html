One leader takes writes; followers copy its log and serve reads

Single-leader replication is the workhorse (PostgreSQL, MySQL, MongoDB, Redis). All writes go to one node, so there are no write conflicts. The leader streams its change log to followers, which gives you read scaling and a fail-over target. The design decision is how many followers the leader waits for before it tells the client "committed".

Replication lag bites users

A user updates their profile, the next page reads from a lagging replica, and the old name comes back. Fixes: read your own writes from the leader for a few seconds; send the last-write position (LSN) with the request and wait for a replica that has reached it; or pin a session to one replica for monotonic reads.

Fail-over, step by step

Detect that the leader is gone (missed heartbeats). Choose the most up-to-date follower, ideally the sync standby. Promote it, fence the old leader so it can't accept writes if it returns, and repoint clients. Managed databases do this in 30–120 s.

What it doesn't solve

Write throughput: every follower replays every write, so replicas add read capacity and nothing for writes. When one leader's writes or disk are the limit, you need partitioning (next slides), not more replicas.
