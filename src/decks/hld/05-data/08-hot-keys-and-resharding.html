Even perfect hashing can't spread one very popular key

Hashing spreads keys evenly, not traffic. A celebrity's profile, a viral post or a flash-sale SKU can send 1,000× the average load to one key, and so to one shard. Detect hot keys, then split them: spread writes across sub-keys, and spread reads across copies.

Detect before it pages you

Track per-key request counts, e.g. top-K sampling in the proxy or redis-cli --hotkeys, alongside per-shard CPU and latency. A shard at 90% while its siblings sit at 20% is a hot key until proven otherwise.

Reshard without downtime

Choose many logical shards up front and map them to fewer physical nodes, so resharding means moving a logical shard rather than rehashing everything. To move one: copy a snapshot, stream the ongoing changes, cut over reads and writes briefly, then verify. Vitess, Citus and DynamoDB automate this.

Design the key for skew

If you know a few entities will be huge (celebrities, big tenants, famous products), treat them differently from day one: a separate code path, dedicated shards, or precomputed fan-out. The news feed design (§15) does exactly this.
