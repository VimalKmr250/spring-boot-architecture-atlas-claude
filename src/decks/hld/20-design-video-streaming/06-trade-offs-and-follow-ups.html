The bill is storage and egress, so trade compute for bytes where views pay it back

Most of the trade-offs here are about money. Keep every rendition of every video hot and storage grows without bound. Encode everything in the most efficient codec and compute costs explode. The answer for both is to let popularity decide: tier storage by age and views, and spend expensive encoding only on videos that will be watched enough to earn it back in egress savings.

Follow-upAnswer sketch
Live streamingIngest over RTMP or SRT, transcode in real time, and publish short CMAF parts (LL-HLS) for 2–5 s latency. Manifests refresh continuously and the CDN caches very briefly.
DRM and piracyEncrypt segments once (CENC) and license through Widevine, FairPlay and PlayReady; use forensic watermarks for leaks.
View countsPlayer events go to a stream processor that dedupes and filters bots; counts are approximate in real time and exact in a daily batch.
Resume where I left offA per-user KV of (video → position), written every ~10 s and read at play time.
Summarise"Resumable uploads go straight to object storage. An event-driven DAG splits each video into chunks and encodes chunks × renditions in parallel on spot capacity, then packages CMAF with HLS and DASH manifests. Playback is signed manifests plus ABR over a tiered, multi-CDN edge. Storage is tiered by age, and costly codecs are used only where views repay them."
