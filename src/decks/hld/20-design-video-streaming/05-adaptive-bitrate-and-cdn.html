Deep dive 2: the player adapts every few seconds, and the CDN keeps the popular head close

Smooth playback comes from two cooperating pieces. On the client, an ABR algorithm chooses the rendition for each next segment from measured throughput and how much video is buffered. In the network, a tiered CDN serves the few popular videos, which get most of the views, from caches near the viewer, and fetches the long tail on demand.

ABR algorithms

Throughput-based algorithms pick the highest bitrate below the recent measured throughput, with a safety margin. Buffer-based ones (BOLA) pick from buffer occupancy, which is more stable. Production players blend both and add switch penalties so quality doesn't oscillate every segment.

Start fast

Time to first frame is what users notice. Fetch the manifest while the title card shows, start at a low rendition with short first segments, preconnect to the CDN host, and serve the first seconds of popular videos from the edge.

Multi-CDN

At tens of Tbps, one CDN is both a single point of failure and a weak negotiating position. Steer each session to a CDN by measured performance and cost, and fail over mid-stream. Segments are plain files, so any CDN can serve them.
