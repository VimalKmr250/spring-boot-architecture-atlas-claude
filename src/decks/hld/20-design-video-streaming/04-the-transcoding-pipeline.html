Deep dive 1: split the video into chunks, then encode chunks × renditions in parallel

Encoding one long file serially on one machine takes about as long as the video itself for every rendition, and a crash means starting over. Instead, split the original at keyframes into short chunks. Each chunk × rendition pair is then an independent, retryable task, and the whole thing finishes in minutes on a large pool of cheap workers.

Why chunking works

Each chunk starts on a keyframe, so it can be encoded without its neighbours. Parallelism turns wall-clock time from "length × renditions" into roughly "one chunk". A failed task retries only its 10 seconds of video, and slow stragglers can be speculatively re-run on another worker.

Per-title bitrate ladders

A cartoon needs far fewer bits than a football match at the same quality. Analyse complexity first and pick each video's ladder (resolutions and bitrates) to match. Encode popular videos in efficient codecs such as AV1 or HEVC later, when viewing numbers justify the extra compute.

Cheap, preemptible compute

Transcoding is batch work that can be retried, which is ideal for spot or preemptible instances at a fraction of the price. Use priority queues so a partner's premiere is processed before a backlog of long-tail uploads, and autoscale the fleet on queue depth.
