If the user doesn't need to wait for it, take it off the request path

In a synchronous chain, latencies add up and availabilities multiply, so every downstream service joins the user's critical path. Putting a queue between the steps that must happen now and the steps that merely must happen is the most useful single move in high-level design.

What you gain

  • Latency: the user waits only for the steps that decide the outcome.
  • Availability: consumers can be down for a while, and messages wait for them.
  • Load levelling: bursts queue up and are processed at a sustainable rate.
  • Decoupling: new consumers subscribe without the producer changing.

What you pay

  • Eventual consistency: the confirmation email may arrive 30 seconds later, so design the UI for it.
  • Duplicates and ordering: at-least-once delivery means consumers must be idempotent.
  • Harder debugging: you need trace ids carried through messages and alerts on dead-letter queues and lag.
  • Don't make a step async when its result decides what the user sees next.
