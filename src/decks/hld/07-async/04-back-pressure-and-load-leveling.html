A queue turns a traffic spike into a backlog you work through at your own pace

Without a buffer, the downstream service has to be sized for the peak. With a queue between them, it only has to be sized above the average. Bursts become queue depth, workers drain it at a steady rate, and the database behind the workers never sees the spike. That is queue-based load leveling.

Back pressure: say "slow down"

When a buffer fills, push back instead of growing without limit. Use bounded in-memory queues, 429 or 503 with Retry-After at the edge, consumers that pull at their own rate, and demand signalling in reactive streams. An unbounded queue just moves the out-of-memory crash somewhere else.

Queues are not infinite

Set a maximum depth or a message TTL, and decide in advance what happens when it's reached: reject new work, shed low-priority work, or spill to cheaper storage. Watch for a queue whose drain rate is below its arrival rate on average. It will never catch up.

Scale consumers on lag

Autoscale workers on queue depth or age rather than CPU (KEDA does this on Kubernetes). But cap the workers at what the downstream can handle: fifty workers hammering one database only moves the bottleneck.
