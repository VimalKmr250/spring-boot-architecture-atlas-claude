Deep dive 2: the router is a policy engine that mostly says "not now"

The best notification system sends fewer notifications. Each candidate passes a sequence of checks, and most of the product value is in them: duplicates dropped, preferences honoured, quiet hours respected, bursts collapsed into digests, and every channel chosen for this user right now. Critical messages skip the checks that could delay them.

Check late, not early

Preferences and quiet hours are evaluated at send time, not when the notification was requested. A deferred or scheduled message rechecks them, so a user who unsubscribed at 9 pm doesn't get the 8 am campaign.

Caps are counters

Per user, per category, per day: a Redis INCR with a TTL until midnight in the user's timezone. Check and increment atomically. Caps are the single best defence against a buggy producer spamming users in a loop.

Aggregation windows

For bursty events (likes, comments), hold the first event for a short window, say 2 minutes, keyed by (user, post, type), and collapse everything that arrives in it into one message. That's fewer notifications, better copy, and lower cost.
