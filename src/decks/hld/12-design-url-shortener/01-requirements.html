Design a URL shortener: a tiny write path and an enormous read path

The classic warm-up question (think bit.ly or tinyurl). It looks trivial, which is the point: the interviewer wants to see whether you find the real problems. Those are generating short unique keys without coordination, and serving redirects in milliseconds at 100× the write rate, with analytics on the side.

Functional

  • Shorten a long URL to a 7-character code.
  • Redirect short.ly/Ab3xK9q to the long URL.
  • Optional custom alias and expiry date.
  • Click statistics per link (may lag by minutes).

Non-functional

  • Redirect p99 < 50 ms, availability 99.99%. A broken link breaks someone else's product.
  • Links are durable: never lost, never re-pointed.
  • Codes are not trivially enumerable.
  • Read-heavy at 100:1; writes may be slightly slower.

Clarify, then scope out

  • "Same long URL twice: same code or new?" Different codes per user is simpler.
  • "Do links ever get edited?" No: immutability helps caching.
  • Out of scope: link previews, QR codes, billing.
