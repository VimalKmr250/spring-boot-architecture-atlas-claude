Deep dive 2: an LSM tree makes writes sequential, and Merkle trees find divergence cheaply

Inside each node, a log-structured merge tree turns random writes into sequential appends. A write goes to a commit log and a sorted in-memory table, which is flushed to immutable sorted files and compacted in the background. Between nodes, replicas drift apart (missed hints, dropped messages), and Merkle trees let two replicas locate the few key ranges that differ without comparing every key.

Durable before acknowledged

A replica acknowledges only after the commit-log append, and the memtable is rebuilt from that log after a crash. Group commit (fsync every few milliseconds for a batch of writes) keeps throughput high. The coordinator's W then counts durable copies, not just copies in RAM.

Three kinds of repair

Hinted handoff fixes short outages. Read repair fixes keys that are read. Anti-entropy with Merkle trees catches everything else, including long outages and bit rot. Each covers what the others miss, and together they make eventual consistency actually converge.

Compaction is the hidden workload

Merging SSTables rewrites data many times (write amplification), competing with foreground traffic for disk bandwidth. Rate-limit compaction, leave 30–50% of the disk free for it, and watch pending compactions: a growing backlog shows up next as slow reads.
