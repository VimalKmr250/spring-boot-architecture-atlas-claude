Design a distributed key-value store: always writable, horizontally scalable, tunably consistent

This is the question that tests the whole fundamentals section at once. Build get and put over hundreds of nodes, with no single point of failure, surviving node and network failures, and letting each caller trade consistency against latency. The standard answer is the Dynamo recipe, used in Cassandra, Riak and DynamoDB's ancestors: partition with consistent hashing, replicate N ways, coordinate with quorums, and repair in the background.

Functional

  • get(key), put(key, value), delete(key); optional TTL.
  • Values are opaque bytes, up to about 1 MB.
  • Consistency chosen per request (ONE, QUORUM, ALL).

Non-functional

  • No single point of failure; add or remove nodes while serving.
  • Highly available for writes, even during partitions.
  • Durable once acknowledged; self-repairing replicas.

Clarify

  • Is strong consistency ever needed, or is eventual with a quorum enough?
  • Range scans? If not, hash partitioning; if so, range partitioning.
  • One datacenter or several? LWW acceptable, or keep siblings?
