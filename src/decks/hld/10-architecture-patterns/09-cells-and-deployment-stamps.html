Cells: copy the whole stack many times so a failure only hits a slice of users

Beyond a certain scale, one big shared system means one big blast radius. A bad deploy, a poisoned cache or a runaway tenant hurts everyone. In a cell-based architecture (Azure calls the units deployment stamps), you run many identical, independent copies of the stack, each serving a fixed subset of customers, behind a thin router.

What you get

A bounded blast radius: a failure or noisy tenant affects one cell. Deploys go in waves, cell by cell, with an automatic halt. Each cell has a fixed, tested maximum size, so scaling becomes "add a cell" rather than pushing one system into untested territory.

What it takes

A partition key that maps cleanly to cells (tenant, account, region). A router that is extremely simple and highly available. Automation to stamp out and upgrade identical cells. And a plan for the rare cross-cell query or tenant migration.

Where you'll see it

Large SaaS (Slack, Salesforce "pods", Azure stamps) and cloud control planes (AWS). In an interview, propose it once the design serves millions of tenants, or when the question stresses blast radius and noisy neighbours.
