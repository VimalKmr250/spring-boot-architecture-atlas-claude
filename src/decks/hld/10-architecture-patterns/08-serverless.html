Serverless: pay per event, scale to zero, and give up control of the runtime

In serverless (functions as a service, plus managed backends), you write handlers and the platform runs them on demand: one isolated invocation per event, scaling from zero to thousands in seconds, billed per millisecond. There are no servers to patch or size. In return you accept cold starts, execution limits and tight coupling to one cloud's services.

Great fits

Event glue (resize on upload, react to a DB change), scheduled jobs, spiky or low-traffic APIs, webhooks, prototypes, and fan-out batch work. It also suits teams without a platform team, since nothing needs patching or capacity planning.

Poor fits

Latency-critical paths that can't tolerate cold starts (unless you pay for provisioned concurrency), long-running or stateful work, WebSockets at scale, and steady high throughput, where always-on containers are cheaper per request.

Design gotchas

Thousands of concurrent functions can exhaust a relational database's connections, so use a connection proxy or a serverless-native database. Make handlers idempotent, since platforms retry. Watch cost at scale, and design for vendor-specific triggers from the start.
