Split by business capability, give each service its data, and budget for the cost

Good microservice boundaries follow the business, not the technical layers. Each service owns one capability (catalog, ordering, payments), its data, and its team. It talks to the others through APIs for queries and commands, and through events for facts. Bad boundaries give you a distributed monolith: every feature needs five services deployed in lock-step.

Finding the boundaries

  • Business capability / bounded context: a capability with its own language and rules. "Product" means different things in catalog and in shipping.
  • Data ownership: exactly one service writes each piece of data. Others read it through the owner's API or keep a copy fed by its events.
  • Team ownership: one team per service (Conway's law works for you or against you).
  • Different forces: separate scaling (search), security or compliance (payments), or release cadence.

The cost ledger

  • Network: latency, partial failure, retries, timeouts on every call.
  • Data: no joins or transactions across services, so sagas, outboxes, read models.
  • Operations: CI/CD per service, containers, service discovery, tracing, a platform team.
  • Contracts: API and event versioning, consumer-driven contract tests.
  • If the teams can't afford these costs, a modular monolith gets most of the boundary benefit.
