Performance, custom collectors, and what's new

The closing questions of a streams round. The last one is where knowing Java 24 and 25 sets you apart from candidates who stopped at 8.

Q25

Are streams faster than loops?

30-second answerNot inherently. For small collections a loop is usually slightly faster, because a stream builds a pipeline and calls lambdas, although the JIT narrows the gap in hot code. Streams can win through laziness and short-circuiting, and in parallel on large CPU-bound work. Choose on readability, and optimise only what a profiler shows is hot, measured with JMH.

Go deeper: The costs that really matter are boxing in numeric pipelines, which IntStream and friends avoid, and misplaced parallelism. A loop is clearer for early exits over complex state, for checked exceptions, and for index arithmetic. A stream is clearer for filter, transform and group pipelines. A hand-rolled System.nanoTime() comparison misleads, because of JIT warm-up and dead-code elimination.

Q26

How would you write a custom Collector?

30-second answerWith Collector.of(supplier, accumulator, combiner, finisher, characteristics…). The supplier creates a mutable container, the accumulator adds one element, the combiner merges two containers for parallel runs, and the finisher turns the container into the result. Declare IDENTITY_FINISH, UNORDERED or CONCURRENT only when they are true.

Go deeper: Offer a real example: a top-N collector backed by a bounded PriorityQueue, which takes O(n log k) time and O(k) memory, where sorted().limit(k) holds everything. Stress the combiner. Sequential streams never call it, so a wrong combiner passes every test until someone adds parallel(). Test custom collectors with a parallel stream. Often a composition of existing collectors, such as collectingAndThen or teeing, is enough.

Q27

What is new in streams since Java 8?

30-second answerJava 9 added takeWhile, dropWhile, the three-argument iterate, ofNullable, and Collectors.filtering and flatMapping. Java 10 added the unmodifiable collectors, Java 12 teeing, and Java 16 Stream.toList() and mapMulti. Java 24 made Stream Gatherers final after previews in 22 and 23: custom intermediate operations, with windowFixed, windowSliding, fold, scan and mapConcurrent built in.

Go deeper: Gatherers are the big one: gather is to intermediate operations what collect is to terminal ones. A gatherer can be stateful, one-to-many and short-circuiting, and optionally parallel. mapConcurrent runs a function on virtual threads with bounded concurrency and keeps encounter order. The most common follow-up is that Stream.toList() returns an unmodifiable list. JDK 25, the current LTS, and JDK 26 left the stream API unchanged.
