Parking lot: the choices to defend, and the follow-ups to expect

An interviewer spends the last fifteen minutes here. They pick one decision and ask why, then add a requirement and watch how much of the design moves. For each follow-up below, the answer names the classes that change. If the answer is "just one", the design is doing its job.

DecisionWhy, and the alternative
Vehicle type as an enumTypes differ only in data. A sealed interface if they start to behave differently.
Free queues per sizeClaiming is O(number of sizes), not O(spots). The alternative, scanning, is fine for 50 spots and wrong for 5,000.
Lock per floorGates on different floors do not contend. One lot-wide lock is simpler and also correct.
Optional for "full"A full lot is a normal outcome, not an error. A duplicate plate or a used ticket is an error, so those throw.
Pricing captured on the ticketA price change never surprises a driver already inside. The alternative, price at exit, needs the lot to say so at the gate.
Injected ClockFees are testable without sleeping, and the demo is deterministic.
Ticket removed before releaseConcurrentHashMap.remove is atomic, so two exit gates cannot both redeem a ticket.
"Two cars grab the last spot at once"The most common concurrency follow-up. Here it is safe: claim is synchronized, so polling the free queue and occupying the spot is one atomic step, and the second car sees an empty queue. The plate check uses Set.add, which is atomic on a concurrent set, rather than a contains followed by an add, which would race. With a database behind it, the same guarantee comes from a conditional update (UPDATE spot SET vehicle=? WHERE id=? AND vehicle IS NULL) and checking that one row changed. See optimistic locking.
Follow-upWhat changes
EV charging spotsA charger flag on ParkingSpot and on Vehicle; the floor keeps charger queues per size and tries them first for EVs. Pricing adds an energy charge as a decorator.
"Nearest to the lift" or "fill top floors first"Extract the floor order and spot choice into a SpotSelectionStrategy. The lot asks it instead of looping in order.
Entrance display boardsFloors publish "spot freed/taken" events; boards subscribe (observer). The lot does not know boards exist.
ReservationsA Reservation holds a spot for a time window; claim skips reserved spots. It becomes the movie-booking hold problem.
Lost ticketLook up the active ticket by plate (the lot already tracks plates) and charge a daily maximum.
Weekend or night ratesA new PricingStrategy, or decorators around the hourly one. No other class changes.
200 lots, one appThe model is per lot; state moves to a database keyed by lot id, and the lock becomes a conditional update. See Scale and distribution.
