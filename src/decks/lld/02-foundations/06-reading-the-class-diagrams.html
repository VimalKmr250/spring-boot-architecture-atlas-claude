The text notation every class diagram in this tab uses

In an interview you draw in a shared editor or on a whiteboard, so the diagrams here are plain text in a small subset of UML. Use the same notation in the interview, and say what an arrow means the first time you draw one.

Draw less

Leave out getters, setters, constructors and toString. Show the fields that carry the design and the methods that do the work.

Arrows point at the dependency

A ──> B means A knows B, and B does not know A. If the arrow goes both ways, ask whether it has to: two-way links are where bugs hide.

Mark the variation points

Every «interface» on the diagram should answer "what might change?" If you cannot say what a second implementation would be, you probably do not need the interface.
