ATM: a machine whose allowed actions depend on what has happened so far

You cannot withdraw before entering a PIN, enter a PIN before inserting a card, or insert a card while another is inside. The ATM problem tests whether you model that with a state machine instead of flags checked in every method. It also tests money handling across two systems: the machine holds the cash, the bank holds the balance, and the two can disagree.

Functional requirements

  1. A customer inserts a card, then enters a PIN. After 3 wrong PINs the ATM keeps the card.
  2. Once authenticated, the customer can check the balance and withdraw cash, several times, then eject the card.
  3. Withdrawals are multiples of 100, at most 10,000 per transaction.
  4. The ATM holds ₹500, ₹200 and ₹100 notes in limited numbers. It refuses an amount it cannot make exactly, before touching the account.
  5. The bank verifies PINs, holds balances and approves debits. The ATM never sees a balance it did not ask for.
  6. If the dispenser fails after the debit, the debit is reversed. A customer is never charged for cash they did not get.
  7. When the ATM runs out of cash it goes out of service and refuses cards.

Questions to ask first

Deposits, transfers?
Not now. The state machine must make them easy to add.
Which bank?
One bank behind an interface. The network switch between banks is out of scope.
Daily limits?
The bank's rule, applied in debit. The ATM has only its per-transaction limit.
Receipts?
A message on screen; a printer is another device behind an interface.
Concurrency?
One customer at a machine. The bank is shared by many ATMs, so it must be safe.

The ordering question to raise yourself"Do we debit first or dispense first?" Dispense first and a failed debit hands out free money. Debit first and a jammed dispenser charges for nothing, unless the debit is reversed. Debit first plus a reversal is the standard answer, and saying it before you are asked is a senior signal.
