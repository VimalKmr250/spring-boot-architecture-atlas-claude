Concurrency in an LLD round: three questions, asked about every piece of shared state

"Make it thread-safe" is the most common follow-up in LLD, and the weakest answer is synchronized on every method. The strong answer starts by naming what is shared, the invariant that must hold, and the smallest set of changes that must happen together. Pick the tool after that.

1 · What is shared?

Which objects can two threads reach at once? A parking floor's free lists, a show's seat claims, a bank account. Objects confined to one thread, and immutable objects, need nothing.

2 · What is the invariant?

The rule that must never be seen broken: a spot has at most one vehicle; a seat has at most one live hold; a balance never goes negative.

3 · What must be atomic?

The smallest group of reads and writes that keeps the invariant: "find a free spot and occupy it"; "check the balance and debit it". That group is your critical section.

Coarse first, then justify going finerOne lock around the whole service is always a correct first answer, and often the right final one. Go finer (per floor, per seat, lock-free) only for a reason you can state: "a release-day show has 500 users on one seat map, and one lock would serialise them all". Every step finer adds a way to get it wrong.
