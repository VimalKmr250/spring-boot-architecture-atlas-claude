Elevator: the choices to defend, and the follow-ups to expect

The demo's last call shows why dispatch must be swappable: under zoning, car A travels from floor 8 to floor 1 while car B sits idle one floor away. Zoning wins at 9 a.m., when everyone comes from the lobby, and loses at 3 p.m. Interviewers want you to say which rule fits which traffic, then show the rule is one class.

DecisionWhy, and the alternative
Dispatch as a strategyThe rule changes with traffic and time of day; the cars do not. Swapped at runtime by useDispatcher.
LOOK inside the carRouting uses only the car's own stops, so it belongs to the car (information expert).
Hall calls assigned onceSimple and predictable. Real controllers re-assign pending calls every few seconds as cars move; a follow-up.
Discrete ticksDeterministic and testable. A real system is event-driven, with timers for travel and doors.
Arrivals as eventsDisplays, chimes and logs subscribe. The car does not know about any of them.
One lock on the systemButton presses, ticks and strategy swaps all serialise. At a few events per second that costs nothing, and it rules out a car being given a stop mid-step.
The listener runs inside the lockIn the demo a listener presses a car button from inside tick, which works because Java's intrinsic locks are re-entrant. A slow listener (a network call to a display) would hold up every car, though. In production, publish events to a queue and let listeners consume them on another thread. It is the same trade-off as synchronous and asynchronous observers.
Follow-upWhat changes
Honour hall-call directionKeep up-stops and down-stops in separate sets; a car serves a floor on the way up only for an up call. step reads the set for its current direction. The dispatcher already accounts for it.
CapacityEach car tracks its load; the dispatcher skips full cars, and a full car ignores hall-call stops until riders get out.
A car out of serviceA serviceable flag, or a State for the car. The dispatcher filters it out; its pending hall calls are handed back to the dispatcher.
Destination dispatchRiders enter their floor in the lobby, so a hall call carries its destination. The dispatcher groups riders by destination, and returns which car to wait for.
Rush-hour switchingA ScheduledDispatcher that delegates to zoning or nearest-car by time of day: a strategy that picks a strategy. No other class changes.
Fire modeA system-wide state: every car cancels its stops and returns to the lobby, and hall calls are refused.
