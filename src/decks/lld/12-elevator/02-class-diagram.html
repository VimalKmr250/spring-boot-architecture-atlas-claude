Elevator: the car routes itself, the dispatcher picks the car, the system owns the clock

Each car owns its floor, direction and sorted set of stops, and decides its next move with LOOK. The dispatcher sees all cars and a hall call, and returns one car; it never moves anything. The system is the controller: it takes button presses, advances time, and tells subscribers what happened.

ElevatorSystem the controller - floors : int - cars : List<ElevatorCar> - dispatcher : Dispatcher - listeners : List<ArrivalListener> - tick : int + hallCall(floor, Direction) : carId + carCall(carId, floor) + tick() + useDispatcher(Dispatcher) + subscribe(ArrivalListener) ◆── 1..* ElevatorCar ──> 1 Dispatcher ──> * ArrivalListener ElevatorCar - id - floor - direction : Direction - stops : NavigableSet<Integer> TreeSet: higher(), lower() + addStop(floor) + step(tick, listener) LOOK: move one floor or stop «interface» Dispatcher + choose(cars, HallCall) : ElevatorCar ┄┄▷ NearestCarDispatcher cost: distance, or detour ┄┄▷ ZonedDispatcher each car owns a band of floors «record» HallCall floor, direction «enum» Direction UP, DOWN, IDLE «interface» ArrivalListener stopped(tick, carId, floor)
ClassOwns the decision
ElevatorCarWhich way to go next, and when to stop
DispatcherWhich car answers a hall call
ElevatorSystemValidating input, advancing time, publishing arrivals
ArrivalListenerWhat to do about an arrival: a display, a log, a chime
Why stops are a TreeSetLOOK asks two questions every tick: is there a stop above me, and is there one below? higher(floor) and lower(floor) answer each in O(log n). A set also merges duplicate presses of the same button for free.
Why the car could be a StateA car idles, moves up, moves down, and (in a fuller version) holds its doors open or is out of service. Here the direction enum is enough, because only step depends on it. Once door states and maintenance arrive, the State pattern is the natural refactoring.
