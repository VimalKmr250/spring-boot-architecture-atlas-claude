Elevator system: two kinds of request, and a scheduling rule that must be swappable

The elevator problem is about separating two decisions. Dispatch: which car answers a call from a floor? Routing: in what order does one car visit its stops? Buildings change the dispatch rule (zones at rush hour, nearest car at night) far more often than they change how a car moves, so dispatch goes behind an interface.

Functional requirements

  1. A building has N floors (0 to N−1) and M cars, each starting on a given floor.
  2. A hall call is a button on a floor with a direction: "floor 4, going down". The system assigns it to one car.
  3. A car call is a button inside a car: "take me to 8". It goes to that car only.
  4. A car keeps moving in its current direction while it has stops ahead, then reverses (the LOOK algorithm). With no stops it goes idle.
  5. The dispatch strategy is configurable and can change while the system runs: nearest suitable car, or fixed zones.
  6. Displays and logs learn about arrivals by subscribing; cars do not know who is listening.
  7. Time advances in ticks: each tick a car either moves one floor or opens its doors.

Questions to ask first

Capacity, weight?
Ignored: every car can always take one more.
Door timing, speeds?
One tick per floor and per stop. Real timings are a follow-up.
Emergency, maintenance?
A car can be taken out of service in the follow-ups.
Destination dispatch?
No: classic up/down buttons. Keypads in the lobby are a follow-up.
Real time or simulated?
Simulated ticks, so the design and the demo are deterministic.

Nouns, and what they become

ElevatorSystemElevatorCar HallCall · recordDirection · enum Dispatcher · strategyArrivalListener · observer

Not classes: button and floor (a button press is a method call; a floor is an int), door (one tick of the car's cycle until door faults are asked for).

Name the algorithmSCAN (the "elevator algorithm" from disk scheduling) runs to the end of the shaft before reversing. LOOK reverses at the last stop, which is what real cars do. First-come-first-served zig-zags and starves distant floors. Naming these three shows you know the trade-off, not just one answer.
