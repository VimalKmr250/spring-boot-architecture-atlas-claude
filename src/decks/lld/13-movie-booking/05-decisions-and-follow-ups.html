Movie booking: the choices to defend, and the follow-ups to expect

Every follow-up on this problem is really "what if this runs on ten servers?" In-process atomics protect one JVM. Across servers, the same three operations (claim if free, take over if dead, release if mine) become conditional updates in a database or in Redis, and the design keeps its shape.

DecisionWhy, and the alternative
Per-seat atomic claimsNo lock, and holds on different seats never wait for each other. The alternative, synchronized on the show, is simpler and fine for one screen; it serialises every user on a hot release.
Claim in sorted orderTwo overlapping requests cannot each take half and both fail. Same rule as lock ordering.
Lazy expiryCorrect the instant a hold lapses, with no job to schedule or monitor. A sweeper can still tidy up.
Charge, then confirm, then refund if lateConfirming is instant and cannot fail except by expiry; charging is slow and can. The reverse order confirms seats that may never be paid for.
Booking-level lock for transitions"Confirm" and "expire" race on the same booking; a small lock makes exactly one win.
Facade serviceThe client calls hold and confirm; the claim protocol stays inside. See Facade.
The same protocol in SQLClaim: INSERT INTO seat_claim(show_id, seat, booking_id) … with a unique key on (show_id, seat); a duplicate-key error means taken. Take over a dead hold: UPDATE seat_claim SET booking_id=:new WHERE show_id=:s AND seat=:x AND booking_id=:old, and check that one row changed. Release: DELETE … WHERE booking_id=:mine. With Redis, SET seat:S1:B1 BK7 NX PX 480000 claims with a built-in expiry. See Distributed locks and leases.
Follow-upWhat changes
Payment callback arrives after a timeoutPayments are confirmed by a webhook, not a return value. Key the webhook by booking id; confirm or refund is decided by tryConfirm exactly as now, and a repeated webhook is ignored because the booking is no longer HELD.
Cancel a paid bookingA REFUND_PENDING state, a refund policy strategy (full refund up to 2 hours before the show), then release the seats.
No single empty seat between bookingsA SeatSelectionRule checked in hold before claiming. It reads the seat map, so it is advisory under concurrency; the claim is still the authority.
Dynamic pricingA PricingStrategy that reads occupancy. The price is fixed on the booking at hold time, so it cannot change during payment.
Coupons and offersDecorators around the pricing strategy, or a discount step in confirm with its own validation.
Live seat map for 10,000 viewersPublish claim and release events; clients subscribe over WebSockets. The map is a read model and can lag; the claim is what counts.
A virtual waiting room at release timeAdmit users in batches before they reach hold. This is a rate limiter in front of the service; see the token bucket.
