A two-week practice plan: an hour or two a day, ending with timed mocks

LLD improves with repetition under a clock more than with reading. The plan below works through the tab in order, then spends the second week solving problems cold, in 45 minutes, before comparing with the solutions. Always compare responsibilities first ("who owns this decision?"), then code.

DayStudyPractise
1FoundationsRewrite one of your own classes with records, sealed types and an injected clock
2SOLID and friendsFind one violation of each principle in code you have written
3Creational and structural patternsWrite builder, decorator and adapter from memory
4Behavioural patternsWrite strategy, state, command and observer from memory; explain the look-alikes aloud
5Parking lot, LibraryRead requirements only; solve in 45 minutes; then compare
6ATM, ChessSame method. For chess, add castling yourself
7Elevator, Movie booking, SplitwiseSame method. For booking, write the 100-thread test first
8ConcurrencyLRU cache and token bucket from memory, with a concurrent test for each
9Scale and distributionFor each of the seven problems, say the shard key and the conditional write aloud
10–13New problems, cold: vending machine, hotel booking, snake and ladder, food delivery, a logger, a file system, a cab-booking app, an online auction45 minutes each, timed, out loud, ideally with a friend playing interviewer and adding a requirement at minute 35
14Interview questionsAnswer each in 30 seconds, then review your weakest two problems
Map new problems to known onesA vending machine is the ATM's state machine. A hotel is movie booking with dates. Snake and ladder is a smaller chess. Food delivery is the elevator's dispatcher with drivers instead of cars. A logger is chain of responsibility plus observers. Name the parent problem in the first minute and borrow its structure.
