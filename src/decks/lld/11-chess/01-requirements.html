Chess: polymorphic rules, and a game state that must never go inconsistent

Nobody expects all of chess in an hour. The interviewer wants six piece types whose movement differs but whose callers do not care, a rule (you may not leave your own king in check) that cuts across all of them, and a game that knows whose turn it is and when it is over. Agree the scope first; castling and en passant are the classic "and now add…".

Functional requirements

  1. Two players, white and black, alternate turns on an 8×8 board, white first.
  2. Six piece types: king, queen, rook, bishop, knight, pawn, each with its own movement. Only the knight jumps.
  3. A move names a from and to square (e2, e4). It is refused if the piece is not the player's, cannot move that way, or would leave the player's own king in check.
  4. Pawns move one square forward, two from their start rank, capture diagonally, and promote to a queen on the last rank.
  5. After each move the game reports check, checkmate (in check, no legal move) or stalemate (not in check, no legal move).
  6. Moves can be undone, one at a time, back to the start.
  7. A game can start from the standard layout or from any position given as text, for tests and puzzles.

Questions to ask first

Castling, en passant?
Not in the code; each is a follow-up with a known shape.
Promotion choice?
Always a queen for now.
Draw rules?
Stalemate only. Repetition and the 50-move rule are follow-ups.
Clock, online play, AI?
Out of scope. The model must not stop them being added.
Input format?
Two squares per move, not algebraic notation.

Not classes: square (a Position plus whatever the board holds there), player (just a Color until players have names, ratings or clocks).

The one rule to design around"May not leave your own king in check" is the only rule that is not about one piece. Do not try to encode it in each piece. Generate each piece's moves ignoring check, try each move on the board, and keep the ones after which your king is not attacked. That single idea handles pins, moving into check and escaping check.
