Chess: pieces generate moves, the game decides which are legal

Each piece answers one question: which squares could I move to from here, ignoring check? Sliding pieces (queen, rook, bishop) share the slide-until-blocked loop; stepping pieces (king, knight) share the one-step loop. That shared loop is a template method in each abstract class, and the subclasses supply only their directions. The game filters for legality and owns turn and status.

Why Piece is sealedThe six types are fixed by the rules of chess; nobody will add a seventh. Sealing documents that, and lets a switch over pieces (for rendering or scoring) be exhaustive. It is the right place for a closed hierarchy.
Why moves are recordsA move stores the captured piece and the piece it placed, so undo restores exactly what was there, promotion included. That is the command pattern. Undo by reversal is cheap; the alternative is copying the whole board before each move (prototype).
Why attacks is separate from targetsA pawn moves straight but attacks diagonally, so "can it move there" and "does it attack there" differ. Every other piece attacks where it can move, so the default implementation reuses targets and only Pawn overrides it.
