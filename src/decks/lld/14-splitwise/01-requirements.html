Splitwise: exact arithmetic, four ways to split, and one real algorithm

Splitwise is a favourite machine-coding problem because it has a precise input format, an exact expected output, and a trap in every line: money that does not divide evenly, splits that do not add up, and debts that should net off against each other. It ends with the question every interviewer asks: "can you settle everyone with fewer payments?"

Functional requirements

  1. An expense has one payer, a total, and a split among participants (the payer may or may not be one).
  2. Split types: EQUAL; EXACT amounts, which must add up to the total; PERCENT, which must add up to 100; SHARES in a ratio (1:2).
  3. Amounts are rupees with paise. Shares must add up to the total to the paisa: ₹100 three ways is 33.34 + 33.33 + 33.33, with the extra paisa assigned by a fixed rule.
  4. Show balances: who owes whom, with debts in both directions between two people netted off.
  5. Settle up: record a payment from one user to another.
  6. Simplify: a list of payments that settles every balance, using as few payments as practical.
  7. Commands arrive as text lines, as in most machine-coding rounds: EXPENSE ana 1200.00 EQUAL ana bo cy.

Questions to ask first

Groups?
One global ledger here; groups are a follow-up.
Several payers?
One payer per expense. Several payers is a follow-up.
Currency?
One currency, two decimal places.
Rounding rule?
Split exactly, then give leftover paise to participants in the order listed.
Edit or delete an expense?
A follow-up: it needs the ledger to be rebuilt from expenses.
