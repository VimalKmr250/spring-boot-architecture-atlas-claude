When one use case spans services: publish through an outbox, coordinate with a saga

In one JVM, the booking facade holds seats, charges the card and sends the email in one method, and compensates on failure. Split into a booking service, a payment service and a notification service, that method becomes a saga: a sequence of local transactions, each publishing an event, with a compensating step for each. The events must be published reliably, and that is the outbox's job.

The dual-write problem tx: UPDATE booking SET status='HELD' commits kafka.send(BookingHeld) crashes before this → the booking exists; nobody heard about it The outbox fix tx: UPDATE booking SET status='HELD' INSERT INTO outbox(event) VALUES ('BookingHeld') one commit: both or neither relay: read outbox → publish → mark sent at least once consumers: dedupe by event id idempotent
Booking saga, choreographed booking HELD ──BookingHeld──> payment charge ──PaymentSucceeded──> booking CONFIRMED ──PaymentFailed────> booking CANCELLED seats released compensation notify on BookingConfirmed: send ticket

Compensation is not rollbackA refund is a new transaction, visible on the card statement, not an undo. Every step needs a compensating action designed up front, and some steps (an email already sent) cannot be compensated, so they go last.
