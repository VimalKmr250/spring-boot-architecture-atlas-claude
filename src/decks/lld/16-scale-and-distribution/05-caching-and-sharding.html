Caching and sharding: find the natural partition key, and cache only what can be stale

Every LLD problem has an aggregate that transactions never cross: a parking lot, a show, a Splitwise group, a building's elevators. That is the shard key, because every invariant lives inside one of them. Caching is then a question of which reads may be a few seconds old. The seat map may be; the seat claim may not.

A hot key cannot be sharded away

One show is one shard, however many servers there are. Spread the load in front of it: a waiting room that admits users in batches (a rate limiter), per-seat keys instead of one show row, and a read model for the map.

Cache-aside, with invalidation on write

Read: cache, then database, then fill the cache. Write: database, then delete the cache entry, so the next read refills it. Expiry (TTL) is the safety net for invalidations that get lost.

Consistent hashing, in one line

Put shards and keys on a ring; a key belongs to the next shard clockwise. Adding a shard moves only about 1/n of the keys, instead of nearly all of them as with hash % n.

How much to say in an LLD roundTwo or three sentences: the shard key and why, what is cached and why it may be stale, and where the hot spot is. The rest belongs to a system-design round, and the Reference deck covers it.
