Patterns that share a class diagram, and the question that tells them apart

Several patterns have almost the same structure: an interface, an implementation, and a class that holds one. What differs is the intent, meaning who creates the object, who changes it, and why. "Isn't that just a strategy?" is a common follow-up, and these are the answers.

PairSame shapeThe differenceAsk yourself
Strategy vs StateA context delegates to an interfaceA strategy is chosen from outside and stays put. A state replaces itself as events arrive.Who decides the next implementation: the client, or the current one?
Strategy vs Template methodVary part of an algorithmStrategy uses composition and can change at runtime. Template method uses inheritance and is fixed per subclass.Do the varying steps belong together, or mix and match?
Strategy vs CommandAn object wrapping behaviourA strategy is how the context does its job. A command is a request, with its receiver and arguments, that can be stored, queued and undone.Would you ever put it in a history list?
Decorator vs ProxySame interface, wraps the targetA decorator adds behaviour and is stacked by the client. A proxy controls access (lazy, cached, remote, secured) and usually manages the target itself.Does the caller know, and choose, the wrapping?
Adapter vs FacadeA class in front of other codeAn adapter makes one interface look like another. A facade makes many classes look like one simpler interface.One object translated, or many coordinated?
Adapter vs BridgeAn abstraction holding an implementorAn adapter is applied after the fact to make existing code fit. A bridge is designed up front to let two hierarchies vary independently.Retrofitting, or planning?
Composite vs DecoratorRecursive composition of one interfaceA composite holds many children and aggregates them. A decorator holds exactly one and adds to it.One wrapped object, or a tree?
Observer vs MediatorObjects are decoupled from each otherObserver broadcasts one-way from a subject to anyone listening. A mediator holds the coordination logic between peers.Is there logic in the middle, or just a list?
Factory method vs Abstract factory vs BuilderCreation is hidden from the callerA factory method picks the class of one product. An abstract factory creates a matching family. A builder assembles one complex product step by step.Which class? Which family? Or which fields?
The honest answer is often "both"The parking-fee rule is a strategy, wrapped in decorators for discounts, created by a factory from the lot's configuration. Naming each role, and why it is there, is a better answer than choosing one label.
