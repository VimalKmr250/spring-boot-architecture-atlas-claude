Every principle as a smell, a fix, and a sentence to say

You will not be asked to recite these. You will be asked why you made a class or an interface, and the answer is usually one of these rows. Learn the smell column: it is how you notice the problem in your own code while the interviewer watches.

PrincipleSmell in your codeFixSay it like this
SRPA …Manager with methods for three concernsSplit by who asks for the change"Pricing changes for business reasons, so it gets its own class."
OCPA switch on a type or mode that grows every monthAn interface at the point of variation"A new rule is a new class; nothing that works gets edited."
LSPAn override that throws, or ignores its inputReshape the hierarchy; prefer immutability"If a subtype has to refuse a method, it isn't that type."
ISPUnsupportedOperationException in an implementationSplit the interface by client"The customer screen never sees the maintenance methods."
DIPnew VendorClient() inside a business classThe policy declares an interface; inject the detail"I can test booking with a fake payment gateway."
DRYThe same rule in two places, changed in oneOne home for each piece of knowledge"The late-fee rule lives in one class."
KISS · YAGNIInterfaces with one implementation and no reason for a secondInline them; add the seam when the need appears"I'll add a strategy when a second rule arrives."
Demetera.getB().getC().doIt()Tell, don't ask: move the behaviour to a"The ticket computes its own fee."
Information expertA service that reads every field of an object to decide somethingMove the method to the object that owns the data"The spot knows whether a vehicle fits."
