A sorted spine that can also drop its oldest element

A monotonic stack discards what is dominated. A monotonic deque does that too, but adds a second eviction rule: elements that have fallen out of the window are removed from the front. Two eviction rules, two ends, and the maximum of every window becomes available in O(1).

The invariant

The deque holds indices whose values are decreasing from front to back. Two consequences follow, and both are used on every step:

  • The front is always the maximum of the current window — no scan needed.
  • Anything smaller than an arriving element is useless forever, because the newcomer is both larger and stays in the window longer.

That second point is the whole justification. A smaller, older element can never be the answer again — so it is not merely delayed, it is dead.

Three moves, always in this order

  1. Expire the front while it has fallen outside the window. This is the rule a stack cannot express.
  2. Evict the back while it is dominated by the arriving value, then push the new index.
  3. Read the front — that is the window's answer.

Each index is pushed once and removed once, from one end or the other, so the total work is 2n — amortised O(1) per element despite two inner loops.

Store indices, never values: the expiry test needs positions.

Applies when

You need the max or min over a sliding range, or a DP transition of the form dp[i] = a[i] + max(dp[j]) over a moving band of j. The deque replaces the inner max with an O(1) read.

Does not apply when

Arbitrary elements are removed, or you need the k-th largest rather than the largest — those want a heap or a balanced tree. Also when the range is not contiguous.

Complexity

O(n) time by push-once, remove-once, and O(k) space for a window of width k. A heap-based solution is O(n log n) / O(n) — say that first, then beat it.

Recognition cues — the literal words in the statement"the max sliding window" · "for each window of size k" · "the absolute difference between any two elements is at most limit" (needs two deques, one for max and one for min) · "at most k steps forward" in a DP · "the shortest subarray with sum at least k" where negatives rule out a plain window. The signal is a max or min over a moving range, evaluated at every position.
