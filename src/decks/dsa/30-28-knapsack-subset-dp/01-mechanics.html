Two dimensions: which items, and how much capacity is left

Every problem here is the same question — choose a subset of items subject to a capacity. The state is two-dimensional, and the two decisions that determine correctness are the iteration direction when you compress it to one array, and the loop order when you are counting.

The state and the transition

dp[i][c] = the best achievable using the first i items with capacity c. Each item offers exactly two options:

  • Skip it — dp[i−1][c], unchanged.
  • Take it — dp[i−1][c − w] + v, if it fits.

That is the whole recurrence. The row index disappears when you compress to one array, which is where the subtlety enters.

The direction rule — the one thing to memorise

With a single dp[] array, the capacity loop direction decides whether each item can be used once or many times:

Backwards
0/1 knapsack — each item at most once. Reading dp[c − w] before it is overwritten means you are reading the previous row.
Forwards
Unbounded — items reusable. dp[c − w] has already been updated this round, so the item can be taken again.

One loop direction, two completely different problems. Getting it backwards produces a plausible wrong number rather than a crash.

Applies when

Choose a subset subject to a numeric budget — a target sum, a weight limit, a count of zeros and ones. The tell is a capacity that the chosen items consume.

Does not apply when

Items are divisible — that is a greedy fractional knapsack, solved by value density. Also when the capacity is enormous, since the table is sized by it; then binary search or maths is needed instead.

Complexity

O(n × capacity) time, which is pseudo-polynomial — it depends on the magnitude of the capacity, not just the input length. Space is O(capacity) after compressing to one array.

Recognition cues — the literal words in the statement"partition into two subsets with equal sum" · "the number of ways to assign + and −" · "the number of combinations that make up that amount" · "the size of the largest subset with at most m zeros and n ones" · "the smallest possible weight of the remaining stone" · "the number of profitable schemes". The signature is a subset choice plus a numeric budget, with the answer being a count or an optimum.
