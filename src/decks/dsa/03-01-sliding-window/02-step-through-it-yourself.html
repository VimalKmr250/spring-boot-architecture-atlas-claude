One move per press — predict it before you click

Each press moves exactly one end of the window by one letter, and says why. Before every press, guess: will the head grow the window, or must the tail shrink it? When your guesses stop being wrong, you have the pattern. Type your own letters to test an idea — pwwkew, aaaa and abcdef are good ones.

Three things to watch for as you pressThe head moves on every letter, no matter what. The tail only moves when a letter is red, and sometimes it has to move more than once — at r = 6 it drops an a it has no quarrel with, because a window has no gaps: to lose the older b, everything in front of it goes too. And the move tape never grows past 2n, however the letters fall.

Now point it at any problem: three questions

1 · What does "fine" mean?

Write down the rule a window must obey. LC 3: no letter twice. LC 904: at most two kinds of fruit. LC 209: the sum is at least the target.

2 · Does growing the window only push one way?

In LC 3 a longer window can go from fine to broken, but never back. In LC 209, with every number positive, it can go from too small to big enough, but never back. That one-way push is Fact 2, and it is what lets the tail move forward for good. With negative numbers a sum can flip back and forth — then it is not a window problem.

3 · What must I remember to check the rule instantly?

That memory is the window state. LC 3: a count per letter. LC 904: a count per fruit type. LC 209: a running sum. Add to it when the head moves; take away exactly the same when the tail moves.

Longest vs shortest — the one flip to rememberFor a longest window (LC 3, 424, 904) the rule breaks by getting too big, so the tail moves while the window is broken and you record once it is fine again. For a shortest window (LC 209, 76) the rule is met by being big enough, so the tail moves while the window is still fine, recording every time — squeezing it as small as it will go. Same caterpillar, opposite reason for the tail to move.
