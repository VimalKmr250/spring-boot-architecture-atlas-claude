A grid is a graph — and the choice of traversal is the answer

Every cell has up to four neighbours, which makes a grid an implicit graph with no adjacency list to build. The only real decision is DFS or BFS, and it is forced by the question: DFS for "what is connected", BFS for "how far".

The choice, and why it is not a preference

DFS
Counting components, measuring a region's size, flood-filling. Order does not matter because you visit everything reachable regardless.
BFS
Shortest path in an unweighted graph, or elapsed time in a spreading process. BFS reaches every cell in non-decreasing distance order, which is a guarantee DFS cannot make.

Using DFS for a shortest path is not slow — it is wrong. It may reach the target by a long route first and report that length.

On a large grid, prefer iterative DFS or BFS: recursion on 10⁶ cells overflows the stack.

Marking visited, and when

Mark a cell when you enqueue it, never when you dequeue it. Otherwise the same cell is added many times before its first visit, and the queue blows up — the classic BFS memory failure.

If the grid may be mutated, overwrite the cell itself and skip the extra array entirely. Say out loud that you are modifying the input; it is usually fine and interviewers like the space saving, but it should be a stated choice.

Unlike backtracking, these marks are never undone — a cell belongs to exactly one component, so revisiting it is always waste.

Applies when

Cells or nodes with neighbours, and a question about connectivity or distance. Grids, mazes, spreading processes, and region counting all reduce to this.

Does not apply when

Edges carry different weights — BFS assumes every step costs the same, so a weighted shortest path needs a priority queue instead. Also when the order must respect prerequisites, which is a different traversal entirely.

Complexity

O(rows × cols) — every cell is enqueued and dequeued once. Space is O(rows × cols) worst case for the queue or the recursion stack, which for a full grid is the entire grid.

Recognition cues — the literal words in the statement"the number of islands" · "the maximum area of an island" · "flood fill" · "the minimum number of minutes until no cell has a fresh orange" · "the length of the shortest clear path" · "a closed island" (surrounded on all four sides — check the border). The word "shortest" or "minimum steps" forces BFS; anything about counting or sizing permits DFS.
