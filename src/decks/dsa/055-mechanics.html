Throw away everything that can never win again

Keep a stack whose values are sorted. When a new element arrives that dominates what is on top, that top can never be the answer for anything further right — so pop it, and settle its answer on the way out. Each element is pushed once and popped once, which is why the whole thing is O(n) despite the inner loop.

The invariant

The stack is always monotone — strictly increasing or strictly decreasing from bottom to top, depending on the question. Everything on it is still waiting for its answer.

A new element a[i] arrives. While it beats the top, the top has found what it was waiting for: pop it and record i as its answer. Then push i.

Anything left on the stack at the end never found an answer — those get the default, usually 0 or −1.

Why it is O(n), not O(n²)

The while loop looks quadratic. It is not: an index is pushed exactly once and popped at most once, so across the entire run the inner loop body executes at most n times in total — not n times per iteration.

Total work is 2n operations, amortised O(1) per element. Being able to state that in one sentence is worth more than the code: it is the follow-up interviewers ask when the loop looks nested.

Store indices, not values. You almost always need the distance i − stack.peek(), and an index gives you the value for free while the reverse is not true.

Applies when

Every element needs to know about the nearest element to one side that is bigger or smaller than it. Also for rectangle problems, where a bar's reach is bounded by the first shorter bar on each side.

Does not apply when

You need the nearest element by position under some other predicate, or the maximum over a sliding window of fixed width — a stack cannot evict from the bottom, which is what that requires.

Complexity

O(n) time by the push-once, pop-once argument. O(n) space worst case, when the input is already sorted so nothing is ever popped until the end.

Recognition cues — the literal words in the statement"the next greater element" · "how many days until a warmer temperature" · "the first element to the right that is larger" · "the largest rectangle" · "the span" · "the nearest smaller value". The giveaway is "nearest" or "first … to the right/left" combined with a size comparison. If the comparison is by position only, it is not this pattern.
