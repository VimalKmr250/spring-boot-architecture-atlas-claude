Keep only k things, and know which one is worst

To find the k largest of n items you do not need to sort n things. Keep a heap of size k, and make it a min-heap — so its root is the weakest of your current best k. Every new item is compared against that root alone: beat it and you are in, fail and you are discarded immediately.

The inversion that trips everyone

For the k largest, use a min-heap. For the k smallest, use a max-heap. This feels backwards and is the single most common mistake in the pattern.

The reason: the root must be the item you are most willing to throw away. When collecting the largest values, the one you would sacrifice first is the smallest of those you hold — so that must sit at the root where it can be inspected and evicted in O(log k).

Java's PriorityQueue is a min-heap by default, so "k largest" needs no comparator at all — which is a useful tell that you have picked the right direction.

The invariant, and the cost

After processing any prefix of the input, the heap contains exactly the best k items seen so far — never more.

Maintaining it costs one comparison against the root, plus an O(log k) push-and-pop only when the newcomer qualifies. Total O(n log k), against O(n log n) for sorting.

When k is small that gap is large — and more importantly the heap needs only O(k) memory, so it works on a stream whose length you never learn.

Applies when

You need the top k by some score, the k-th element, or a running best over a stream. Also when items arrive continuously and the total count is unknown, which rules out sorting entirely.

Does not apply when

You need the full sorted order (just sort), or k is close to n (sorting is simpler and no slower). Also when a count bounds the values — bucketing by frequency is O(n) and beats the log factor.

Complexity

O(n log k) time, O(k) space. Quickselect gives O(n) average for a single k-th query but O(n²) worst case and destroys the input; a heap is the safer default and the one to offer first.

Recognition cues — the literal words in the statement"the k-th largest" · "the top k frequent" · "the k closest" · "return any k of them" · "design a class that returns the k-th largest so far" (a stream, so O(k) memory is mandatory) · "repeatedly take the two heaviest". The phrase "k-th" next to a large n is the clearest signal in this deck.
