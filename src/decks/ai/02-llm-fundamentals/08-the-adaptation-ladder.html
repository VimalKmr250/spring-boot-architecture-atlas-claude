Five ways to make a model fit your problem, cheapest first

When a model's answers are not good enough, there is a ladder of fixes. Each rung costs more and changes something different. Most enterprise problems are solved on the first three rungs; teams that jump straight to fine-tuning usually had a knowledge problem, which fine-tuning does not fix.

Knowledge problem

"It does not know our return policy." Fix with RAG. Fine-tuning a model on your policy documents teaches it the style of your policies, not reliable recall of their contents, and it is out of date the day a policy changes.

Live-data or action problem

"It does not know where order 1042 is" or "it should cancel the order." Fix with tools: the model asks, your code calls the order service with the user's permissions.

Behaviour problem

"It knows the facts but answers in the wrong format, tone or level of detail." Try a clearer prompt and examples first. Fine-tune when you have thousands of good examples and the prompt has become long and expensive.

Say it in the interview"RAG for knowledge, tools for live data and actions, fine-tuning for behaviour. I start at the cheapest rung and only climb when an evaluation set shows the current one is not enough." The full decision table is on Prompting, RAG or fine-tuning?.
