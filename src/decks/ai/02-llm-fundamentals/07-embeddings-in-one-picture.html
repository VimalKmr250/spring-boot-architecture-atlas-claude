An embedding turns meaning into a position, so "similar" becomes "near"

An embedding model reads a piece of text and returns a fixed-length list of numbers, for example 1,536 floats. Texts with similar meaning land close together, even when they share no words. That one property powers semantic search, RAG, deduplication, clustering and recommendations. The full treatment is in Embeddings.

Cosine similarity

The usual measure of "near": the cosine of the angle between two vectors. 1 means the same direction (same meaning), around 0 means unrelated. Most embedding models return vectors of length 1, so cosine and dot product give the same ranking.

Why not keyword search?

"Reimbursed for a broken lamp" shares no keywords with "refund for a damaged item". Embeddings match meaning, synonyms and paraphrases, across languages if the model is multilingual. Keyword search still wins on exact codes, SKUs and names, which is why production systems often combine both.

Embedding models are not chat models

They are smaller, much cheaper per token, and produce vectors, not text. You must embed documents and queries with the same model: vectors from two models live in different spaces and cannot be compared.
