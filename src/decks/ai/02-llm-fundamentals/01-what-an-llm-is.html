An LLM predicts the next token, over and over, and nothing else

A large language model is a neural network trained on a huge amount of text to answer one question: given these tokens, what token is likely to come next? Instruction tuning and human feedback then teach it to follow instructions and hold a conversation. Everything that looks like reasoning, memory or knowledge comes out of that one loop.

It is a function, not a database

Text in, text out, nothing kept between calls. It does not look facts up; it produces text that is likely given its training. That is why it can be fluent and wrong at the same time.

Its knowledge is frozen

The weights stop changing at a training cutoff. Your orders, your policies and yesterday's price change are not in there, and never will be. You supply them in the prompt: that is what RAG and tools do.

Every word costs a step

Input tokens are processed in parallel, so a long prompt is relatively cheap in time. Output tokens are produced one at a time, so a long answer is slow. Ask for short, structured answers when you can.

Your application does the rest

Memory, retrieval, tool execution, validation, security and retries all live in your code. Spring AI's job is to make that code look like the rest of your Spring application.

Say it in the interview"An LLM is a next-token predictor: stateless, trained once, with knowledge frozen at a cutoff. So I treat it as a reasoning and language engine, and I bring the facts, the memory and the actions from my own systems."
