Full tuning, adapters and distillation: what changes, and who runs it

All fine-tuning starts from a pre-trained model and nudges its weights with your examples. The methods differ in how many weights change, what hardware they need, and whether you or a provider runs the training and serves the result. As a Java team you will usually call a hosted tuning API or a platform team's pipeline, then point Spring AI at the resulting model id.

MethodWhat it isTypical use
Supervised fine-tuning (SFT)Train on input → ideal output pairsFormat, style, classification, extraction
LoRA / QLoRASFT that trains small adapter matrices onlyOpen-weight models on modest hardware; many tenants share one base
Preference tuning (DPO and similar)Train on "this answer is better than that one"Tone, helpfulness, refusing the right things
DistillationA strong model produces the training labels for a small oneCutting cost and latency on a high-volume task
Hosted tuningProvider APIs: OpenAI fine-tuning, Bedrock custom models, Vertex AI tuningNo GPUs to run; data goes to the provider; served at their prices
Self-hostedOpen weights tuned with Hugging Face tooling, served by vLLM or OllamaData must stay in-house; you run training and serving

Catastrophic forgetting

A model tuned hard on one task can get worse at others. Evaluate the tuned model on general prompts as well as the target task, and keep adapters narrow.

Training data leaks

Models can reproduce training examples verbatim. Anything in the training set, such as a customer's address in a support reply, may come back out. Redact before training, not after.

Where Java fits

Building and governing the dataset from your systems, calling the tuning API or pipeline, registering model versions, and serving the tuned model through Spring AI like any other.
