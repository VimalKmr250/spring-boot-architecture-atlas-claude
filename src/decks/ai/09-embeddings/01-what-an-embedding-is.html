A fixed-length vector that encodes meaning, compared with a distance metric

An embedding model maps text (or an image) to a point in a space with hundreds or thousands of dimensions. The individual numbers mean nothing on their own; only distances between vectors matter. The model was trained so that texts people would call "about the same thing" end up close together, which is exactly what search and retrieval need.

MetricFormulaNotes
Cosine similaritya·b / (|a| |b|)The default. Angle only, ignores length. 1 = same direction.
Dot producta·bSame ranking as cosine when vectors are normalised to length 1, which most hosted models do. Fastest.
Euclidean (L2)|a − b|Straight-line distance; smaller is closer. Equivalent ranking for normalised vectors.

What "similar" means is the model's choice

Embeddings capture topic and intent well. They are weaker on negation ("refund" and "no refund" are close), exact numbers and identifiers, and anything the model's training did not cover, such as your internal product codes.

Scores are not probabilities

0.82 does not mean "82% relevant", and a good threshold for one model is wrong for another. Pick thresholds from labelled examples of your own data, per model.

Embed what you will search for

A chunk of text, a product title plus description, a ticket subject plus first message. Embedding a whole 50-page document as one vector averages away the detail you will want to find.
