The embedding model is a long-term commitment; choose it with your own queries

Swapping a chat model is a configuration change. Swapping an embedding model means re-embedding every stored document, because vectors from different models cannot be compared. So the choice deserves a small experiment: 50–100 real questions, the passages that answer them, and a measurement of how often each candidate model puts the right passage in its top 5.

What to compare

Retrieval quality
recall@5 and MRR on your question set; public benchmarks are a starting point only
Languages
multilingual models if users ask in one language about documents in another
Max input
must exceed your chunk size, or chunks are silently truncated
Dimensions
storage and index memory scale linearly with them
Where it runs
data residency often decides between a public API, your cloud's model service, or self-hosting
Price
per million tokens, times the size of your corpus, times how often you re-embed
Storage back-of-envelope
1,000,000 chunks × 1,536 dims × 4 bytes  ≈ 6.1 GB of raw vectors
same, shortened to 512 dims               ≈ 2.0 GB
HNSW index on top                         ≈ 1.5–2× the raw size, ideally in RAM
re-embedding 1M chunks × 400 tokens       = 400M tokens per full rebuild
Plan for the migration you will needStore the model name and version with every vector (as metadata), keep the source text so you can re-embed, and build a new index alongside the old one before switching queries over. Then a better model next year is a background job, not an outage.
