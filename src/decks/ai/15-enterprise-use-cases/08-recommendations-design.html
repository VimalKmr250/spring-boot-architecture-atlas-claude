Recommendations: retrieve candidates cheaply, let the model order and explain a few

A recommender has two stages. Candidate generation narrows a catalogue of 100,000 products to 50 plausible ones: embeddings of what the customer viewed and bought, plus business filters (in stock, not already owned, allowed in their region). Ranking orders the shortlist. An LLM adds value only in the second stage and only on a short list: re-ranking with context a vector cannot see ("buying for a small flat", "matching the oak table they bought") and writing a one-line reason per item.

Where the LLM pays

On high-value, low-volume surfaces: a "complete the room" email, a stylist-style assistant, a B2B reorder suggestion with reasons. On a product page served millions of times a day, embeddings and rules alone are usually the better trade.

Explanations change behaviour

"Matches the oak dining table you bought in March" builds trust and click-through, but only if it is true. Generate reasons from the customer's actual history, passed in the prompt, and never from guesses.

Privacy and fairness

Use only data customers agreed to for personalisation; keep sensitive attributes out of prompts and filters; offer "why am I seeing this?" and an opt-out. Monitor that recommendations do not systematically exclude groups or regions.
